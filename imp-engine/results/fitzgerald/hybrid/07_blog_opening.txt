Creative burnout comes to nearly all of us once, and it comes the way the rain cooled that afternoon to a damp mist, through which occasional thin drops swam like dew and a faint doubt seemed to settle over the bleared windows. You cannot catch the hour it began—only that the work you once loved as an extraordinary gift has turned its face away, and you sit there drained, uninspired, a stranger at your own desk. The world offers no mercy, with its colossal appetite for production exacting some contributory emotion from you each day, and some quiet toll from whatever in us used to sing. And yet the light is not out. Burnout is no sentence; it is only a season, and seasons turn. In what follows we'll trace where the dread first gathers, learn to read its small grotesque warning signs, and reach—patient, against the current—back toward the far green light of the work that was always, and is still, your own.
