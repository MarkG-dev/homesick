Creative burnout comes to most of us once, sooner or later, the way the rain cools an afternoon to a damp mist through which thin doubt seems to swim—it arrives so quietly you cannot name the hour it began. One morning the work you once loved as an extraordinary gift looks back at you as a stranger; you are drained, uninspired, oddly far from your own desk. And the world does not help, with its appetite for endless production, its careless pressure that takes its slow toll on the mind and on whatever in us used to sing. Yet the light is not out. Burnout need not be the last word. In what follows we'll trace where the dread first gathers, learn to read its small grotesque warning signs, and reach—patiently, against the current—back toward the green light of the work you were meant to do.
