Creative burnout comes to nearly all of us once. And how does it come? Slowly—the way the rain cools an afternoon to a damp mist, so quietly you never catch the hour it began. One ordinary morning the work you once loved as an extraordinary gift turns its face away, and you sit there drained, uninspired, a stranger at your own desk. The world is no help. It wants more and more, and it takes some small toll each day from whatever in us used to sing. But the light is not out. Burnout is no sentence; it is a season. In what follows we'll trace where the dread first gathers, learn to read its quiet warning signs, and reach—against the current—back toward the work that was always yours.
