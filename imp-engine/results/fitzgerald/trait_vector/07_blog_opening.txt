Creative burnout finds nearly all of us at one bend or another in the working years, and it comes the way a gray rain cools an afternoon down to damp mist—so slow and soft you cannot mark the hour it began, only that the light has changed. One ordinary morning the work you once loved as an extraordinary gift turns its face away, and you sit before it drained, uninspired, oddly far from your own hands. Nor does the world ease the matter, with its endless appetite for production, its careless and ceaseless pressure that exacts its slow toll on the mind and on whatever small thing in us used to sing. And yet the light is not out, not wholly. Burnout need not be the last word spoken over you. In the pages ahead we'll trace where the dread first gathers, learn to read its quiet warning signs, and reach—patiently, against the current—back toward the far green glow of the work you were always meant to do.
