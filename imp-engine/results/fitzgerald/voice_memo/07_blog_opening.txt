Creative burnout comes to almost everyone once, and it comes the way the rain cools an afternoon to a damp mist, so quietly you never catch the hour it began—only that the light has gone thin and doubtful over everything. One unremarkable morning the work you loved as an extraordinary gift turns its face from you, and you sit there drained, uninspired, a stranger at your own desk. The world is no help, with its restless hunger for more and more, its careless pressure exacting some small toll from the mind each day, and from whatever in us used to sing. And yet the light is not out. Burnout is not a sentence, only a season. In what follows we'll trace where the dread first pools, learn to read its quiet warning signs, and reach—patient, against the current—back toward the green glow of the work that was always yours.
