Creative burnout comes to nearly everyone once, and it comes the way the rain cools an afternoon to a damp mist, so quietly you never mark the hour it began—only that the light has gone thin and doubtful over the work. One unremarkable morning the thing you once loved as an extraordinary gift turns its face from you, and you sit there hollowed, uninspired, a stranger to your own hands. The world offers no mercy, with its restless appetite for more, exacting some small contributory toll each day from whatever in us used to sing. And yet the light is not out. Burnout is no sentence; it is a season, and seasons turn. In the pages ahead we'll trace where the dread first pools, learn to read its quiet warning signs, and reach—patient, against the current—back toward the far light of the work that was, and remains, your own.
