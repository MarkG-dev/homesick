Creative burnout comes to nearly all of us once, and it comes the way the rain cooled that afternoon to a damp mist, through which occasional thin drops swam like dew and a faint doubt seemed to settle over everything. One ordinary morning the work you once loved as an extraordinary gift turns its face away, and you sit there drained, uninspired, a stranger at your own desk. The world is no help, with its colossal appetite for production exacting some contributory emotion from you every day, and some small toll from whatever in us used to sing. And yet the light is not out. Burnout is no sentence—only a season. In what follows we'll trace where the dread first gathers, learn to read its quiet warning signs, and reach, against the current, back toward the green light of the work that was always yours.
