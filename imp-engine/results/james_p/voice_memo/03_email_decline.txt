Your invitation reached me, and I find I must answer it with the one word I least wished to use. It is not that Thursday is impossible in any absolute sense—few things are—but that another claim has settled itself across the hour, and I have not, when I examine the matter honestly, the means of dislodging it. I should be sorry, sorrier than the occasion strictly warrants, were you to read in this a coolness; the coolness is all in the circumstance, and none of it in me. Send me, if you will, whatever the meeting leaves behind it—the notes, the residue, the things that get decided when one is not there to watch them being decided—and let us, before the wish has time to thin into mere politeness, find some other hour. I have noticed that the meetings one misses acquire, in the missing, a weight the attended ones somehow never do; whether that is regret, or only the imagination at its usual work, I could not undertake to say.
