One puts the Aura Pro on, and the curious thing—the thing the catalogue cannot quite get at—is that nothing announces itself. There is no silence dropped over you like a cloth; there is rather a slow recession, almost a tactful one, of all that had been pressing without your having noticed it pressed, until the music stands forward as though it had been waiting, in another room, for the door to be opened. Thirty hours, they say, the battery will give you; a comfort, too, of the kind one registers only by its persistence, never by its arrival. But the real account of the thing is harder, and I am not certain I can give it: it is the difference between the music coming at you and your coming, at last and undistracted, to it. Whether you would work, or travel, or merely sit—and I have never been sure those are as separate as we pretend—the instrument asks only that you attend; and what attention discovers, when it is no longer fought for, is not perhaps the music at all, but oneself, restored to the capacity for it.
