I write to you a stranger, and conscious of being one, because something has been forming in me as I have watched your work—watched it longer than this note can decently account for—that I find I cannot quite keep to myself: a sense that between what you are doing and what we are doing there waits a possibility neither of us, kept separate, could bring off alone. I will not call it certainty. Certainty, at this distance, would be a kind of flattery, and the wrong kind. I will only say that my attention to you began as something less than interest and became, by degrees and almost against its will, something more. Whether the thing I half-see would survive being talked about, I genuinely do not know—and that, precisely, is what a short conversation next week, at whatever hour least disturbs you, might be left to discover. I should want to hear your own aims before I presumed to speak to them; for I have come to think that the partnerships worth anything are rarely the ones either party felt sure of going in.
