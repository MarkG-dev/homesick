It is not, the sufferer comes at last to perceive, that he has ceased to love the work, nor that the work has done anything he could fix upon as wanting; it is rather that some finer faculty, the very medium through which the loving used to pass, has by slow degrees gone out of it. The knowledge comes without bitterness, as such knowledge, at a certain age, is apt to come—rather as a faint coolness comes in at an opened door, perceptible only to those who had been warm. This is what we have agreed to call creative burnout; and the pressure that breeds it—incessant, impersonal, indifferent to the slow rhythms by which anything of worth is made—performs upon us a work we are slow to perceive and slower to forgive. He, who had fancied himself the manager of his own gift, comes to suspect he had been, from the threshold onward, the most assiduous of its dupes. And yet one comes to wonder whether what one flees, in such a season, were ever the thing one had any real hope of leaving behind. In what follows I shall try to set out—not a cure, of which I am suspicious, but a manner of attending—how the thing comes on, by what signs it may be known, and by what patient adjustments one finds, if a way remains, the road back.
