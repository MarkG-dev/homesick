It is not, we have come to feel, that the Aura Pro silences the world—silence would be its own coarse imposition; it is rather that the noise is persuaded, by degrees so fine the ear scarcely marks them, to withdraw, until the music stands forward as a thing one had been, until that moment, surrounded into forgetting. There is a battery to sustain you through some thirty hours; there is a comfort one registers not at first but later, in its mere persistence; there is a clarity one is half tempted to call disinterested. Whether you would work, or travel, or merely sit—and the distinction, we suspect, is less absolute than the catalogue pretends—the instrument renders sound not as a thing flung at you but as a faint coolness comes in at an opened door, perceptible only to those who had been warm. One comes to wonder, indeed, whether what one had been wanting were the music at all, and not the recovered capacity to attend to it.
