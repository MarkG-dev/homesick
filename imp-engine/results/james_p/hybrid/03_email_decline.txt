It is not, you will understand, that Thursday's meeting fails to draw me—the drawing is, if anything, the trouble; it is rather that a prior claim has laid itself across the hour with a firmness I am in no position to dispute. I would not have you read in the absence anything so coarse as indifference. Send me, if you will, whatever the deliberations leave behind them—the notes, the residue, the things decided when one is not there to watch them decided—and let us, before the wish has had time to attenuate into mere civility, find another hour. It is a curious truth, I have come to feel, that the meetings one misses acquire, in the missing, a weight the attended ones somehow never come to possess.
