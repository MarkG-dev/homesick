I venture to address you—a stranger, and conscious of the presumption—because there has formed in me, over a longer watching of your work than this brief note can well account for, a conviction I find I cannot decently keep to myself: that between your enterprise and my own there waits an arrangement of mutual advantage which neither, in isolation, could contrive. I will not flatter you, or not grossly; I will only say that what you have built has been observed, here, with an attention that was not at first interested and became so, by degrees, almost against its own intention. Whether the thing I dimly perceive would survive the test of being spoken of, I cannot at this stage pretend to know; that is precisely what a conversation—a brief one, at whatever hour next week should least disturb you—might determine. I should wish to understand your own ends before presuming upon them, and to learn, before I offer, where an offering might honestly fall. It is a curious truth, that the partnerships worth having are seldom the ones one is most certain of at the outset.
