It was with a pleasure I should be sorry to understate that I received your invitation to Thursday's gathering; and it is therefore with a corresponding reluctance—not, you will understand, the reluctance of indifference, but rather its precise opposite—that I find myself obliged to decline. There has arisen, in the disposition of that particular day, a conflict I am at no liberty to dissolve; the thing, in short, will not be moved. Pray do not suppose the matter to weigh lightly with me. I should be grateful, more grateful than the small request can express, to be kept within the circle of whatever the meeting brings to light—any notes, any residue of its deliberations—and I should like, before too long an interval has been suffered to attenuate the intention, to find another hour in which we might speak. It is a curious thing, the way an absence, when one truly regrets it, makes itself more present than the attendance ever could.
