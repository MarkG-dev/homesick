I address you, a stranger and aware of the liberty I take, because there has formed in me—over an observation of your work more prolonged than this note can reasonably explain—a conviction I find I am not at liberty to suppress: that between your undertaking and my own there subsists a possibility of mutual advantage which neither, kept apart, could realise. I will not say I am certain of it; certainty, at this distance, would be the cheaper because the more dishonest claim. I will say only that what you have built has been watched here with an attention not at first interested and become so, by degrees, almost despite itself. Whether the possibility I imperfectly perceive would survive the discipline of being talked over, I do not pretend to know; it is exactly this that a conversation—brief, and at whatever hour next week should least encroach upon you—might be left to decide. I should wish to understand your ends before presuming to address them, the order of those operations being, I have come to think, the whole difference between a proposal worth making and one merely made.
