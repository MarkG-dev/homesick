It is not, I would maintain against the readier accounts, that one has ceased to care for the work, nor even that the work has in any measurable particular altered; it is rather that the faculty by which caring was used to be conducted has, by a process too gradual to be dated, attenuated, so that what once presented itself as appetite presents itself now—when it presents itself at all—as a fatigue one is tempted, wrongly, to read as a verdict. This is the condition we have agreed, for want of a finer term, to call creative burnout; and the agreement, like most such, both clarifies and conceals. The pressure to produce without intermission—a pressure indifferent to the slow and irregular rhythms by which anything of worth is in fact arrived at—performs upon us a work we are slow to perceive and slower to forgive. It is not, I would insist, a sentence. What I shall attempt in what follows is less a remedy, of which I am constitutionally suspicious, than a manner of attending: by what signs the thing announces itself before it has done its worst, and by what unhurried adjustments one may make, if a way at all remains, the long return.
