It would be too crude to say merely that the Aura Pro cancels the noise about you, the term cancellation implying an abruptness foreign to the actual operation, which is rather a gradual and almost imperceptible withdrawal of the world's intrusion, so that what one is left with is not silence—silence would be its own kind of pressure—but the music, presented at last without competition. The advantage is not, we would insist, a matter of mere capacity, though the capacity is considerable, some thirty hours of it; nor of mere comfort, though comfort there is, of the unobtrusive sort one notices chiefly in its persistence; it is rather the difference, harder to name and therefore more worth naming, between hearing a thing and being permitted to attend to it. Whether the occasion be labour, or transit, or repose—and the distinctions among these, we suspect, are less absolute than the catalogue pretends—the instrument consents to render sound as something one has not so much consumed as understood.
