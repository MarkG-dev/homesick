There was a thing which came upon one in the course of the work which one could not, for a long while, name; it was not that one had ceased to love it, nor that the work had altered in any particular which one could fix upon, but rather that some finer faculty, the very medium through which the loving had used to pass, had by degrees too gradual to be dated gone quietly out of it. The knowledge came without bitterness. It came, at a certain age, as such knowledge will. It came, indeed, as a coolness comes in at an opened door, perceptible only to those who had been warm; and this we have come to call creative burnout, though the name, like most names for what goes on within one, both eases and conceals. The pressure which breeds it—incessant, impersonal, indifferent to the slow rhythms by which anything of worth comes to be made—does upon one a work which one is slow to perceive and slower to forgive; one had supposed oneself the manager of one's own gift, and comes to feel one had been, from the threshold onward, its most assiduous dupe. Whether there were a way back, I could not now say. What follows is not the cure, but the attending: how the thing comes on, by what signs it may be known, and by what patient adjustments one might, if the way were still open, begin to find it again.
