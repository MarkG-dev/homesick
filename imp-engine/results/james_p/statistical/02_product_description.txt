There was a thing the Aura Pro did which one could not, at first, have named; it was not that it shut the world out, for shutting-out would have been a coarse and noticeable act, but rather that the noise, the whole press of it, came by slow degrees to withdraw, until what one had all the while been surrounded by was simply, and without announcement, gone. The music came forward then. It came as a thing which had only been waiting. There was a battery which would carry one, they supposed, through some thirty hours; there was a comfort which one came to feel not at first but later, in its mere persistence; there was a clearness which one could, were one exact, call far more than ordinary. Whether one would work, or travel, or merely sit—and the difference among these mattered far less than one supposed—the sound came in as a coolness comes in at an opened door, perceptible only to those who had been warm; and what it gave back, one could not finally be sure, was the music, or one's own lost way of attending to it.
