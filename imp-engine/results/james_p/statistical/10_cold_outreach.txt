There was a thing which had brought me to write to you, a stranger, which I could not, with any confidence, call a certainty; it was not that I held it for settled that we ought to work together, but rather that a possibility had formed in me, by slow degrees and over a longer watching of your work than this note could well account for, that between your enterprise and my own there waited something which neither, kept apart, could come to realise. My attention to you had begun as something less than interest. It had become, almost against its own intention, something more. Whether the thing which I half-perceived would survive the being talked over, I could not now say—and that, precisely, was what a brief conversation next week, at whatever hour should least disturb you, might be left to determine. I should wish to hear your own ends before I presumed upon them; for the partnerships worth the having, I came to feel, are seldom the ones of which either party felt most sure at the outset.
