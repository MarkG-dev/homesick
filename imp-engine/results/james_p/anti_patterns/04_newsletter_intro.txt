We will not greet you, in the worn manner of such pages, with the announcement that we are glad you are here; the gladness, if we have it, had better show in something other than its declaring. It is not that we presume to know what your week has been—productive, the formula would have us ask, and we have grown suspicious of the word; it is rather that, whatever it was, you have come back to us across it, and the fact alone is worth more than the inquiry. What we have done is to gather: the stories, the half-signs, the developments that are never developments while they happen and become so only later, in the slow light of an afterthought. Take it up over the morning's cup, or in some snatched interval of a day already too full, and find in it—if you find anything—not the news, which lies everywhere about, but the rarer occasion of an attention you might not else have spared. What in all this will prove to matter, and to whom, we would not, at this stage, undertake to say. Let us begin.
