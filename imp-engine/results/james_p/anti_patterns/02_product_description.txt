We will not tell you, in the flat manner of the catalogue, that the Aura Pro is loud in its silence; the claim would mistake the very nature of the thing. It is not that the world is shut out—shutting-out being a coarse operation; it is rather that the noise is induced, by degrees the ear can scarcely register, to withdraw, until the music stands forward as though it had only been waiting for the intrusion to cease. There is a battery that sustains, we are assured, through some thirty hours; there is a comfort one perceives not at first but later, in its mere persistence; there is a clearness one hesitates, almost, to call disinterested. Whether you would work, or travel, or simply sit—the distinction is less absolute than one supposes—the sound arrives as a faint coolness comes in at an opened door, perceptible only to those who had been, until then, surrounded. What it restores, one cannot finally be sure, is the music, or only one's own forgotten capacity to attend to it.
