It was not, the sufferer presently came to feel, that he had ceased to love the work, or that the work had done anything he could have fixed upon as wanting; it was rather that some finer faculty had, by degrees, gone out of it. The knowledge came to him without bitterness, as such knowledge, at a certain age, was apt to come—rather as a faint coolness comes in at an opened door, perceptible only to those who had been warm. This is the thing we have agreed to call creative burnout, and the pressure that breeds it—incessant, impersonal, and indifferent to the slow rhythms by which anything of worth is made—works upon us, he came to suspect, as the subtlest form a discouragement could assume. He, who had fancied himself the manager of his own gift, came at last to wonder whether he had been, from the threshold onward, no more than the most assiduous of its dupes. And yet: he came to wonder, too, whether what one fled in such a season were ever, in the nature of the case, the thing one had any real hope of leaving behind—or whether the way back were not, all along, the same door by which the coolness came. In what follows I shall try to make out, as a man pieces a torn letter, how the thing comes on, by what signs it may be known, and by what patient adjustments one finds the road home.
