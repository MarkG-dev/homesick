It was not, the listener presently came to feel, that the Aura Pro had done anything so coarse as to shut the world out; it was rather that the noise, the whole insistent press of it, had been persuaded to withdraw—by what art, and to what fine degree, one was at no early stage prepared to say. There were moments, indeed, when its perfect quiet struck one as the subtlest form an attentiveness could assume: thirty hours of it, a comfort one noticed chiefly in its persistence, a clearness that came without bitterness, as such clearness, to the practised ear, was apt to come. Whether one would work, or travel, or merely sit—the distinction mattered less than one supposed—the music arrived at last, not as a thing flung at one, but rather as a faint coolness comes in at an opened door, perceptible only to those who had been, until then, surrounded. One came to wonder whether what one had been missing, all the while, were the music at all, and not rather one's own lost capacity to attend to it.
