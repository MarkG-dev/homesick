I write to you a stranger, and aware of the liberty in it, because something has been forming in me as I have watched your work—watched it, I will own, rather longer than this note can decently account for—that I find I am not at liberty to keep to myself: a sense that between what you are about and what we are about there waits a possibility neither of us, kept apart, could bring to anything. I will not call it a certainty; certainty, at this distance, would be the cheaper because the more dishonest claim. I will say only that my attention to you began as something less than interest and became, almost against its own intention, something more. Whether the thing I half-perceive would survive the discipline of being talked over, I genuinely do not know—and that, precisely, is what a brief conversation next week, at whatever hour least disturbs you, might be left to determine. I should wish to understand your own ends before presuming upon them; for I have come to suspect that the partnerships worth the having are seldom the ones either party felt most sure of at the start.
