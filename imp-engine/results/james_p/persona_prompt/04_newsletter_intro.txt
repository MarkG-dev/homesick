There is a small marvel in your having come back to us at all, and it is the marvel, rather than the welcome, that I would rather dwell on. I will not ask whether your week was a productive one; the question has worn thin, and I am no longer certain I should know what its answer meant. What we have done, on your behalf, is to attend: to gather the stories, the half-formed signs, the developments that are never quite developments while they are happening and become so only later, in the slow light of a second thought. Take this up where you will—over the morning's first cup, or in some snatched interval of a day already overfull—and find in it, if you find anything, not the news, which lies everywhere about and grows cheaper as it lies, but the rarer occasion of an attention you might not otherwise have spared yourself. Let us begin. What in all of it will prove to matter, and to whom, is precisely the thing neither of us can yet, in honesty, claim to know.
