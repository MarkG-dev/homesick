There is more in your invitation to Thursday's meeting than I can conveniently say, and rather more in my having to decline it. It is not that the hour is, in any absolute sense, impossible—few hours are; it is that a prior claim has laid itself across this one with a firmness I am in no position to dispute, and I would not have you read in the fact anything so coarse as a want of wishing to come. Send me, if you will, whatever the deliberations leave behind them—the notes, the residue, the small things that get settled while one is not by to watch them settled—and let us, before the wish has thinned into mere civility, contrive some other hour. I have come to suspect that the meetings one misses take on, in the missing, a weight the attended ones somehow never quite acquire; though whether that is regret, or only the imagination at its accustomed work, I should not undertake to say.
