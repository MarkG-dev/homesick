The whole of the thing happens not in any event but in a slow alteration of what one perceives, and it is there, accordingly, that I would have us look. It is not that one has ceased to love the work, nor that the work has changed in any particular one could put a finger to; it is that some finer faculty, the very medium through which the loving used to pass, has by degrees too gradual to be dated gone quietly out of it. The knowledge comes without bitterness, as such knowledge, at a certain age, is apt to come—rather as a faint coolness comes in at an opened door, perceptible only to those who had been warm. This we have agreed to call creative burnout; and the pressure that breeds it—incessant, impersonal, indifferent to the slow rhythms by which anything worth doing is in fact arrived at—performs upon you a work you are slow to perceive and slower to forgive. You had fancied yourself the manager of your own gift, and come to suspect you were, from the threshold onward, its most assiduous dupe. Whether there is a way back I would not be so bold as to assert; what follows is not the remedy but the attending—how the thing comes on, by what signs it may be known before it has done its worst, and by what patient adjustments one might, if the way is still open, begin to retrace it.
