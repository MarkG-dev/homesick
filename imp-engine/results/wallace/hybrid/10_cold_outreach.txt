Subject: A Proposition (and an Honest Admission About What That Word Usually Means)

Here is the thing about cold outreach emails — and you know this, you have read approximately four hundred of them, each one beginning with *I hope this email finds you well*, which is a sentence that finds no one well, that is in fact the textual equivalent of a waiting-room magazine from eight months ago — the thing is that they are almost always about the sender wanting something while performing the grammar of mutual benefit, and the performance is so consistent, so technically proficient, that the person receiving the email has long since lost the ability to distinguish the genuine article from the imitation, assuming the genuine article exists, which is the question I am sitting with right now as I write this to you.

My name is Alex. I am not going to tell you this email finds you well.

What I am going to tell you is that I have been following what you are building — not in the cursory, pre-outreach, tab-open-for-forty-seconds way, but in the way where something a person made causes you to stop and think *oh, someone actually thought about this* — and that we are doing something adjacent enough that a conversation between us might produce something neither of us would produce alone, which is either the definition of collaboration or the sales pitch for collaboration, and I am aware those are not always the same thing.

The emotional paradox at the center of an email like this one — the thing neither party names, the thing that makes the whole exchange faintly exhausting — is that I am asking you to trust me at the exact moment I have given you no particular reason to, and we both know it, and the knowing doesn't dissolve the ask so much as it just hangs there, acknowledged, between us.

So here is what I am actually proposing: a call, thirty minutes, no deck unless you want one, in which I listen more than I talk and we find out whether the overlap between what you are doing and what we are doing is real or whether it is the kind of overlap that looks real from a distance and then, up close, turns out to be two people standing in different rooms through a window.

I think it is real. I could be wrong about this.

If you are open to it, I would genuinely like to find out.

— Alex
