Remote work is not, it turns out, a perk—which is how companies still talk about it, "perk," as though the ability to work without a two-hour commute that costs you four hours of living is equivalent to a ping-pong table—but a baseline expectation, the new floor, and the organizations that have not figured this out yet are not being bold or traditional or principled; they are simply losing, slowly and then faster, the specific kind of person who is good enough to have options, which is to say the person they most need, which is to say this is not really about flexibility at all but about whether you understand what year it is.
