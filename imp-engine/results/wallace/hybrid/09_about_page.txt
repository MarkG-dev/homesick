Brightpath was founded in 2021 on a premise that sounds obvious until you sit with it long enough to realize it is not obvious at all — that technology, the thing that was supposed to simplify your life, the thing you downloaded and subscribed to and paid the monthly fee for, has become, for most small business owners, not a solution but a second job, a sprawling, notification-dense, update-required second job that runs parallel to the actual job and that nobody, when they were imagining owning their own business, imagined signing up for.

What we are — engineers, designers, people who have spent enough time inside broken systems to know exactly where the breaks are — is a team that finds this particular failure embarrassing, not in the abstract but personally, the way you find something embarrassing when you were part of the industry that produced it and you know how it happened and you cannot pretend otherwise.

So the mission, if you want to call it that — and mission is a word we use carefully, because mission has been used to mean almost nothing, to mean we have a Slack channel and some core values on a poster — is narrower and more specific than empowerment, which is a word that tends to mean less the harder you press on it: we want to build tools that work the first time, that do not require a tutorial, that a person running a bakery or a landscaping company or a three-person consulting shop in Akron can pick up on a Tuesday morning without losing the Tuesday morning.

We are three years in. There is more to build than we have built. That is not a problem. That is, actually, the whole point.
