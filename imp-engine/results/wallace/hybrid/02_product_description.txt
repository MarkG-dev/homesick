The Aura Pro is, on its surface, a pair of headphones — which is what you'd call them to someone who asked, headphones, thirty hours of battery, active noise cancellation, yes they're worth it — but what they actually are, what they actually do, is something that takes a little longer to explain and that you may find either clarifying or uncomfortable depending on how honest you're willing to be about why you wanted them in the first place.

Here is the thing about noise cancellation, the real thing, which is not in the spec sheet: when the Aura Pro's active noise cancellation engages — and it engages fast, faster than you expect, a kind of soft implosion of the world — what you feel is not silence exactly, not the absence of sound, but something more like the removal of a pressure you had stopped noticing was there, the office's HVAC system, the seat-back rattle of a 737 somewhere over Denver, the specific frequency of other people existing near you, and what replaces it is a room of your own making, which is either exactly what you needed or a very precise model of something lonelier, depending, as I said, on the day.

The sound itself — the thing the headphones are ostensibly for, the music or the podcast or the phone call you've been putting off — is crystalline in the way that word actually means something here and not just marketing, each frequency sitting in its own space the way well-mixed audio is supposed to sit, the bass present but not compensating for anything, the highs extended without becoming the kind of brightness that makes you wince after an hour, which is to say the Aura Pro is doing the thing it promises to do, and the thirty hours of battery life means it will keep doing it for a full day and then most of another, longer than most people's actual patience for being alone with whatever they've chosen to listen to.

Premium comfort, the draft says, and what this means materially is: memory foam ear cushions that learn the specific geometry of your head, an adjustable headband that does not, after two hours, make you aware that you are wearing a headband, a weight — 250 grams — that you will forget about, which is the whole point, which is what comfort means when it's working, the disappearance of the object into the experience.

What the Aura Pro gives you, finally, is a choice — not between noise and quiet, but between the world as it arrives and the world as you'd prefer it, which is a choice that feels like freedom and is freedom, more or less, with the small asterisk that you are the one who has to decide, every time you put them on, which version you actually want. That part, they cannot do for you. The headphones just make the question harder to ignore.
