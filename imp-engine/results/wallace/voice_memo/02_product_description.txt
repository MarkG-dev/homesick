There is a moment — you have probably had it, or something close enough to it that the distinction barely matters — when the noise of a given Tuesday becomes too much. Not dramatic noise. Not sirens or catastrophe. Just the low, cumulative, fluorescent-lit noise of other people existing near you on an airplane or in an open-plan office or in the specific purgatory of a coffee shop where someone is on a call about "synergies" and another person is playing a video without headphones, which, fine, okay, we are all just trying to get through the day.

The Aura Pro is, technically, a pair of headphones. Thirty hours of battery life. Industry-leading active noise cancellation. Premium drivers that do something genuinely unusual with the midrange frequencies, something that makes a well-recorded piano sound like a piano and not like the idea of a piano. The fit is the kind of fit that makes you forget you are wearing them, which is either the highest possible praise or a slightly unsettling thing to say about an object you have placed on your head.

But here is what they actually are, or what they actually do, which is not quite the same thing: they make a pocket. A small, pressurized pocket of chosen sound inside the larger unchosen noise of being alive in proximity to other people who are also alive and also making noise and also, probably, overwhelmed. You put them on and the world does not disappear — you are not deluded, you know the guy with the synergies call is still there — but it recedes to a distance you can bear.

That distance. It turns out that is the whole thing.

The Aura Pro. Hear what you actually want to hear.
