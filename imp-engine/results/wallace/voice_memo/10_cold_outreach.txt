Subject: This is a cold email (and I want to be honest about that)

Here is the thing I keep thinking about, and I apologize in advance for the thinking: most cold emails — including, if I'm being rigorous, this one — are performing a kind of sincerity rather than enacting it. The writer says *I hope this finds you well*, which is a phrase that has been rubbed so smooth by use that it no longer means anything, that functions now as a kind of throat-clearing, a way of saying *I am a professional person who learned to begin emails this way* without actually hoping anything about your wellness at all.

So. I'm Alex. This is a cold email. I've been following what you've built — genuinely, not in the way people say *genuinely* to signal that they're about to say something they don't entirely mean — and there's something in it that made me stop and think we might be able to do something real together, something that would matter to the people paying attention to both of us, which is a way of saying our audiences but feels more honest said this way.

I don't know exactly what that looks like yet. That's the truth. What I'd like — if you're open to it, and I understand completely if you're not, this is a cold email, you owe it nothing — is twenty minutes on a call next week to find out whether the thing I'm imagining is a real thing or just the idea of a thing, which are, I've found, not always the same.

You can tell me no. But I hope you won't.

Alex
