There is a particular anxiety — call it the anxiety of the inbox, which is not really about email at all but about the accumulated weight of everything you have agreed, by subscribing, to eventually read — that arrives every time another newsletter does, and this one is no exception, which is to say: you are already doing the thing, already scanning for permission to stop, already negotiating with yourself about whether "keeping up with the industry" (a phrase that deserves to be held at arm's length, anatomized, asked what exactly it thinks an industry is and whether anyone has ever successfully kept up with one) constitutes genuine work or the performance of it. We are, I should say — and the "we" here is doing real labor, standing in for a small number of people who also cannot stop reading things they did not have time to read — complicit in this. The roundup exists because the anxiety exists; the anxiety exists because the roundup exists. That is not a paradox so much as a business model.

What follows is this week's most consequential material, or at least the material that seemed most consequential at the time of assembly, which is itself a qualifier worth sitting with. Some of it will matter to you. Some of it will produce the specific emotional texture of relevance-without-application — the feeling of being informed in a way that changes nothing — and you will close the tab anyway, which is fine, which is what tabs are for.

The week did not wait for us to be ready.
