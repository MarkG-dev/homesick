There is a meeting — the Thursday planning meeting, which the calendar app (Outlook, presumably, that particular shade of corporate blue that has colonized every open-plan office from Canary Wharf to the Salesforce Tower in San Francisco) has flagged in my schedule as a "conflict," which is to say: two obligations have been placed in the same temporal slot by a system that does not experience obligation and cannot, therefore, feel the specific compound texture of wanting-to-attend-while-being-unable-to, that particular administrative guilt — and I cannot be there.

I want to be clear about who is doing the declining here. I am. Not a scheduling conflict, not a prior commitment (that phrase, "prior commitment," which implies hierarchy without naming it, which lets the calendar absorb the agency that belongs to a person), but me, making a choice under constraint, which is what all choices are, or at least most of them.

The thing I would ask — and I recognize I am asking, not merely "requesting," the way a form requests your date of birth — is that someone send notes afterward, so that the meeting, having happened without me, can at least reach me in transcript form, which is its own kind of attendance, or something like attendance.

And perhaps we find another time. The offer stands. Which is to say: I am still here, still interested, still caught in the same institutional machinery that generated this meeting in the first place.
