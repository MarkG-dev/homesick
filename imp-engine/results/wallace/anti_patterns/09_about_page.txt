Brightpath — which is, let us pause here and name what it is, a "belief statement" dressed up as a company name, the kind of compressed aspiration that venture decks require and that everyone in the room knows is doing the work of an argument no one has actually made — was founded in 2021 by engineers and designers and people who had, presumably, sat inside enough large systems to understand what those systems want, which is not simplicity but the appearance of simplicity, not tools that work but tools that make the not-working feel like your fault. The thing that drives us, or at least the thing we are willing to say drives us (and naming the difference between those two is, we recognize, the whole problem), is the conviction that small businesses — the woman running a Fresno catering operation on a secondhand Dell, the man maintaining three spreadsheets where one database should be, the customer who has been told to "just use the software" by someone who has never used the software — deserve technology that does not require them to become a different kind of person in order to operate it. We value transparency, which is a word that means we will tell you what we are doing; we value collaboration, which means we will ask you what you need; we will put you first, which means we have already caught ourselves, just now, using "you" as though we were not also the people who built the thing you are trying to use, which is to say: we are not outside this. We are just getting started. And the starting never quite ends.
