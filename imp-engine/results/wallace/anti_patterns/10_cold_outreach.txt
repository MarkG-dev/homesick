Subject: A collaboration — or something like it

There is a particular experience — the exact emotional texture of reading someone's work and recognizing, with the slightly uncomfortable clarity of catching your own reflection in a shop window on Mercer Street, that the thing they are building is the thing you have been circling around from the other side — and I want to name that experience here, upfront, because naming it is the only honest way I know to begin.

My name is Alex. That "I," I should say immediately, represents a company with its own interests, its own metrics, its own quietly humming machinery of partnership goals — which is to say you should hold the "I" loosely, the way you hold any first-person claim that arrives unsolicited in your inbox.

We have been watching what you have built — watching is perhaps the wrong word, too passive, too surveillance-adjacent, but "following" implies a kind of fandom that undersells the analytical attention we have actually paid — and the conclusion we keep arriving at, the one that survives the skepticism we apply to our own enthusiasm, is that our audiences are not the same audience and are also, somehow, the same audience; which is the condition that makes collaboration worth the awkwardness of an email like this one.

The awkwardness being: we both know what this email is. You have read emails like this before. I have written emails like this before, or at least something like writing them, and we are both, right now, performing the ritual of pretending the ritual is not a ritual.

And yet.

What I am proposing — a call, next week, thirty minutes, the kind that presumably has an agenda but actually has a question — is less a pitch than an admission that there is a problem neither of us can solve alone: the problem of reaching people who already trust someone else.

The question is whether that someone else could be both of us at once. I do not intend to answer that here. That is what the call is for.
