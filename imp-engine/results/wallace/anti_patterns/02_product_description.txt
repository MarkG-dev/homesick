The Aura Pro — which is what we are calling it, "Pro," that suffix the consumer electronics industry deploys the way a realtor deploys "cozy," to signal aspiration while foreclosing inquiry — arrives as the company's most technically ambitious noise-canceling headphone, a device whose active noise cancellation system does not merely reduce the world so much as it makes a considered institutional decision, somewhere inside its firmware, that the world is not worth your attention right now.

And perhaps it isn't.

Thirty hours of battery life, which means the Aura Pro can outlast a transatlantic flight from JFK to Singapore with approximately six hours remaining — six hours during which you will, presumably, have run out of podcasts and begun to sit with the exact emotional texture of what it means to be suspended at 38,000 feet inside a machine that has also decided, in its own way, to cancel the noise. The over-ear cushions exert the specific pressure of something that wants to be comfortable and knows it, which is either reassuring or unsettling depending on what you brought to read. The acoustic drivers render the low end of a Coltrane record with the same fidelity they render a conference call, which is the Aura Pro's central, unresolved tension — the thing it cannot quite say about itself, the part where "tailored to your lifestyle" means it will hold, with equal care and equal indifference, whatever you ask it to hold.

You put them on. The world recedes. You catch yourself wondering what, exactly, you just agreed to — and the Aura Pro, its noise cancellation humming with the quiet confidence of a system that has already made up its mind, does not answer.
