There is a meeting on Thursday — a planning meeting, specifically, the kind convened in a room that smells faintly of the previous meeting's coffee and whatever ambient anxiety that previous meeting failed to resolve — and I want to be there, or rather I want to want to be there, which is the more honest formulation and also, I recognize, a slightly maddening one to receive in a decline email.

The conflict is real — not philosophical, not the polite fiction that "conflict" sometimes launders into respectability, but an actual prior claim on the same block of hours, the sort of scheduling collision that the calendar app (mine is the default iOS one, unoptimized, a small confession about how seriously I take my own logistics) renders in that clinical red overlap that somehow still fails to prevent the guilt.

What I want you to understand — and I'm aware that "what I want you to understand" is doing a great deal of performed sincerity here, the kind of sincerity that knows it's performing and does it anyway because the alternative is a blankness that helps no one — is that being excluded from a room where decisions are made, even when you are the one doing the excluding, even when the exclusion is logistically justified, carries its own small particular weight: the paradox of opting out of a process you care about precisely because you care about other things, the arithmetic of limited hours being, in the end, the most democratic and least consoling fact about anyone's week.

Please send the notes. I mean that without qualification. And let's find another time — not a placeholder, not the scheduling equivalent of "we should catch up sometime," but an actual time — to connect on whatever the room decides.
