Subject: A partnership worth the awkwardness of a cold email

Here is the performative contradiction I am walking into willingly: this is a cold outreach email, engineered — presumably by some committee of best-practices blog posts and conversion-rate optimists — to feel warm, and I am sending it anyway, because the alternative is not sending it, and not sending it forecloses the thing I actually want to discuss.

My name is Alex, and I have been watching what you've built — not in the vague, flattering way that cold emails typically mean when they say "following your work," but in the specific, slightly embarrassing way where you find yourself noticing the decisions: the positioning, the audience you've chosen to trust, the things you've declined to do that your competitors do loudly and badly.

What I want to propose is a partnership between our two companies — not a synergy, not a value-add, not a strategic alignment (the words that arrive pre-emptively deadened) — but a collaboration, the kind where both audiences receive something they would not have received otherwise, which is either the whole point or nothing at all.

You know the moment I mean, and I mean it as genuinely as the form allows: the moment you recognize that someone else has already solved, or is solving, the adjacent problem — the one that sits just outside your lane but close enough that its absence costs you something you can't quite invoice.

That is the moment I am writing from.

I would like a call next week, not jogs — not a "quick touch-base," the distinction matters here — but a real conversation, thirty or forty minutes, where I can learn what you are actually trying to accomplish and say plainly how we might help.

The offer is modest. The potential is not.

— Alex
