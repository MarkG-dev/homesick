Brightpath was founded in 2021 on a premise that is either obvious or radical depending on how much time you have spent — and I have spent too much, and probably so have you — watching a small-business owner in, say, a 900-square-foot dry cleaner on the kind of commercial strip that exists in every zip code click through seven screens of enterprise software engineered, presumably, by some committee that had never needed to close a register at midnight alone: that technology should reduce complexity rather than perform the reduction of complexity while quietly adding more of it underneath. We are engineers, designers, and what the founding documents optimistically call dreamers — not visionaries, the distinction matters — who believed that the tools handed to small businesses were built for a customer who didn't quite exist, a frictionless rational actor unbothered by the gap between the product's promise and the forty-five minutes it actually takes to onboard. The emotional paradox we are trying to address is precise: being told you are empowered by a system that has made you dependent on it. What we build instead — the dashboards, the integrations, the unglamorous but load-bearing infrastructure of a business that needs to just function — is meant to be transparent in the sense that you stop noticing it, the way you stop noticing a door that opens when you push it. We are, in 2024, still early enough that the shape of what we're becoming is not yet fixed, which is either a warning or an invitation, and we are genuinely, if a little nervously, unsure which.
