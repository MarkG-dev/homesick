There is a problem with the SmartBrew — not a rumor, not a risk modeled in a liability spreadsheet and quietly absorbed as acceptable, but a specific, material defect present in a subset of units that we, having identified it, are now required by the logic of our own stated values to name out loud, which is what this statement is attempting to do, however imperfectly, however aware we are that the act of drafting a public apology is itself a genre with its own cosmetic conventions — the word *sincerely*, the phrase *top priority*, the soft bureaucratic cushion of *inconvenience* — that tend to insulate the writer from the weight of what is actually being said.

So let us try again, more precisely: your SmartBrew, the one on your kitchen counter in whatever city you live in, the one you filled this morning or didn't fill because something felt off, may be one of the affected units — and we are asking you to stop using it, not as a precaution engineered, presumably, by some committee balancing reputational exposure against actual harm, but because the harm is real, the risk is thermal, and the machine should not be trusted until it has been replaced or refunded, whichever you prefer, both of which we will provide without condition.

The emotional paradox here — and it would be dishonest not to name it — is that we built a device meant to be the first thing you trusted in the morning, the automatic and reliable thing, the thing that did not require your judgment at 6 a.m., and we are now asking you to exercise judgment about that very device, which is a particular kind of betrayal, the kind that hides inside helpfulness.

Visit our website. The process is straightforward, or we intend it to be. Your trust, which we have not yet fully earned back, is the thing we are working toward, not the thing we are claiming.
