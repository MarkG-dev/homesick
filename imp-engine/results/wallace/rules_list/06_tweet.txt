Remote work is here — not arriving, not trending, not being piloted by some forward-thinking VP of People in a WeWork on Flower Street — here, present tense, already the baseline against which every RTO memo reads as a kind of grief, and you know this, I know this, even the companies mandating badge-ins by 9 a.m. Tuesday know this, which is why the mandate exists at all: not confidence but its opposite. The organizations that treat flexibility as a feature will get the people who have options; the ones treating the office as a loyalty test will get the people who don't — or the people who leave anyway, quietly, over eighteen months, one LinkedIn update at a time. That's not a prediction. It's already the ledger.
