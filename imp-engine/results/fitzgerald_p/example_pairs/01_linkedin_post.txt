For five years Northwind had the particular gift of making each of my mornings feel like the one it had been waiting for, and it was a generous fiction, the kind that costs the teller little and warms the listener for years. Now I am going across town to Lumen to take up the product, and I leave behind mentors who handed me their confidence the way the rich hand over a loan they never expect repaid. I have loved the work in a thin, hopeful way, the way a poor man loves a house he passes on his walk home, and told myself nearness would be enough. It is, until it isn't. The lights of one office go down behind me; another comes up gold across the dark, and I drive toward it privately certain it will burn a little brighter — knowing, in the same breath, how these things go.
