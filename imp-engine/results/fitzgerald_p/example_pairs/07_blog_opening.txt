Creative burnout creeps up the slow polite way that accounts close: not in a single afternoon but in a quiet cooling, until one morning you sit down to the work you once loved like a face across a crowded room and find the music gone, and cannot say when it stopped. The world keeps asking us to produce and produce, the way it once asked the beautiful only to dance, until the dancing hollows us out, luminous and emptied and far from the joy that started it. But burnout is weather and not a verdict; it gathers, and it lifts. Here we will trace how it comes on, how to read its first gray sky, and how a tired hand finds the road back to the work that once felt like being young.
