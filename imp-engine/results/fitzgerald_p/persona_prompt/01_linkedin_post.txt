I have spent five years at the edge of the Northwind floor, watching the work go by in its gold light the way one watches a dance one is not quite in, and now I am crossing town to Lumen to lead the product there, and the strange grief of any leaving is that it shows you, all at once, the brightness you only ever half belonged to. To the mentors and colleagues who lent me their certainty when I had none of my own, who gave it the way the rich give a loan they never expect repaid, I owe a debt I will carry like the memory of one good summer. I took all of it gladly. The lights of one house go down behind me and another comes up across the dark, and I drive toward it the way a man always drives toward the next lit window, certain it will burn warmer. It rarely does. But one goes.
