Creative burnout comes the way the end of summer comes, not in one grieving afternoon but in a slow cooling of the light, until one ordinary morning you sit down to the work you once loved like a face across a crowded ballroom and find the music gone, and cannot say when it stopped. The world keeps asking us to produce and produce, the way it once asked only the beautiful to dance, until the dancing hollows them, luminous and emptied and strangely far from the joy that started it. But burnout is weather, not a verdict; it gathers, and it lifts. Here we will trace how it comes on, how to read its first gray sky, and how a tired hand finds the road back to the work that once felt like being young. Nothing golden stays. But tended, some of it comes round again.
