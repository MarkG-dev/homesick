There is a particular hour, late in a Northwind spring, when the office windows go the color of weak champagne and a man understands that five years have passed the way money passes through generous hands, and I find that mine have passed and I am leaving them. I am going over to Lumen to take up the product, and to the colleagues and the patient mentors who taught me everything I have, who lent me their certainty when I had none of my own, I owe a debt I will carry the way one carries the memory of a good summer. I learned from each of you, and learning, I have come to think, is the only inheritance that does not spoil. The lights at Northwind are still burning as I close the door, and they will burn for someone else now. We always believe the next house will be brighter. It usually is, for a while.
