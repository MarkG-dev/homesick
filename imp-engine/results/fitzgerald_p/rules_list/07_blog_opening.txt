Creative burnout comes the way the end of summer comes, not in a single afternoon but in a slow cooling of the light, so that one morning you sit down to the work you once loved like a face across a ballroom and find that the music has stopped and you cannot remember when. The world asks us to produce and produce, the way it once asked the beautiful to dance and dance, until the dancing hollows them out, luminous and exhausted and disconnected from the joy that started it all. But burnout, I have come to believe, is a season and not a sentence. Here we will trace how it gathers, how to read its first gray weather, and how a person finds the road back to the work that once felt like being young. Nothing golden stays. But some of it, tended, returns.
