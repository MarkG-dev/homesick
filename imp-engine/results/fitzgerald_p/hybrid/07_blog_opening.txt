Creative burnout comes the way the end of a long summer comes, not in a single grieving afternoon but in a slow and almost tender cooling of the light, so that one ordinary morning you sit down to the work you once loved like a face across a crowded ballroom and find, with a quiet worse than any pain, that the music has gone and you cannot say when it stopped. The world keeps asking us to produce and produce, the way it once asked the beautiful only to dance, until the dancing hollows them out, luminous and emptied and strangely far from the joy that first set them moving. But burnout is weather and not a verdict; it gathers, and it lifts. Here we will trace how it comes on, how to read its first gray sky, and how a tired hand finds again the road back to the work that once felt exactly like being young.
