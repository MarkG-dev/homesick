I stood at the rim of the Northwind floor on my last gold evening there and watched five years go past me the way money goes through generous hands, lightly and all at once, and I understood that I was leaving it for Lumen, where I will lead the product, and that leaving is only the arithmetic by which a man learns at last what he is about to lose. To the mentors and the colleagues who lent me their certainty when I had none of my own, who handed it over the way the rich hand over a loan they never expect repaid, I owe a debt I will carry like the memory of a good summer. I took everything they offered. The lights of one office go down behind me and another comes up across the dark, and I drive toward it privately sure it will burn a little brighter — knowing, in the same breath, that they never quite do.
