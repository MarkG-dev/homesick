The Aura Pro has thirty hours of battery life, which means it will outlast your flight, your meeting, your difficult phone call with your mother, and still have enough charge left to get you through dinner. This is not nothing.

Here is what the Aura Pro actually does: it cancels noise. Not politely, not tentatively, but with the kind of commitment most of us reserve for avoiding people we owe apologies to. The active noise cancellation is industry-leading, which is a phrase that usually means very little, except in this case it means you will put these headphones on in a open-plan office and the open-plan office will cease to exist. You will still be in it. But it will cease to exist.

The sound is clear. Genuinely, specifically clear — the kind of clear where you hear something in a song you have listened to four hundred times and you think, oh, so that was always there. The fit is comfortable enough that you forget you are wearing them, which is the only thing that matters about comfort and the thing no one ever says directly.

You can use the Aura Pro while working, while traveling, while relaxing, while doing whatever it is you tell yourself you are doing when you are actually just trying to be alone for twenty minutes. It does not judge. It simply plays the music. That is, honestly, more than most things offer.
