Let me tell you something nobody warned me about when I decided to make creativity my whole personality: it will betray you. Not dramatically, not all at once — that would almost be merciful. No, it does something sneakier. It drains out of you like a slow leak you don't notice until you're standing in front of a blank canvas, or a blinking cursor, or whatever altar you've built your identity on, and you feel absolutely, devastatingly nothing. I've been there. Face-down in the creative void, wondering if the person who used to love this work ever actually existed or if I just made her up. That's burnout, baby — and it's not a personality flaw, it's not laziness, and it is not the end of your story. It's a signal. A loud, obnoxious, impossible-to-ignore signal that something in the system is broken. In this post, I'm going to walk you through exactly what causes creative burnout, how to catch it before it hollows you out completely, and — the part I actually care about — how to crawl your way back to work that feels like *yours* again.
