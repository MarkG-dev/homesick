Declining meetings is the one social contract I have learned to honor by breaking it. Last Thursday I stood in my kitchen for eleven minutes deciding whether the colander goes on the top rack or the bottom, and that is the level of executive function I would bring to your planning meeting. I know this about myself. I keep moving anyway.

I cannot make Thursday. Thursday is already a thing (a bad thing, a fixed thing) and I am the scheduling conflict — not a vague prior obligation but the specific, recurring problem of having said yes to too many Thursdays in a row until they collapsed into one long Thursday from which I have not recovered.

I want to stay in the loop. I mean that. I want the notes, the action items, the small summary email with three bullet points and a fourth bullet point that is somehow both urgent and vague. I want to be the kind of person who reads those emails on the same day they arrive. I read them eventually.

Please share whatever comes out of it. I will absorb it in the way you absorb a conversation happening one room over — partially, warmly, with genuine goodwill and structural limitations that are entirely my own fault.

Let's find another time. There is always another time, until there isn't, and that is the only honest thing I can tell you about scheduling.
