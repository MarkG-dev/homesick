Here is a personal law I have arrived at through experience: every collaboration I have ever talked myself out of pursuing turned into someone else's best year.

I know this because I have the emails to prove it — the ones I drafted and deleted, the ones sitting in a folder labeled "Drafts" like a small museum of my own hesitation, the ones I replaced with nothing, with silence, with the ambient hum of doing things alone.

So I am sending this one.

My name is Alex. We have been watching what you have built — not in a vague, flattering way, not in the way you say you have been watching someone when you mean you glanced at their website once. We have been watching the way you watch something that makes you think: there is a version of what we do that looks like that. The work is specific. The work is considered. The work is the kind of thing that makes our own work feel like it has somewhere to grow toward.

I will confess the unflattering part: I do not have a fully formed proposal to hand you. I have a strong instinct that our audiences overlap in ways that would reward both of us, a few ideas that are half-baked but promising (the best ones usually are), and the conviction that a twenty-minute call would do more work than this email can.

That is the whole pitch. The instinct, the ideas, the call.

Would next week work for you?

— Alex
