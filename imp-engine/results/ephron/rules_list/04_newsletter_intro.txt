The best newsletters assume you have time. You do not have time. Nobody has time, and yet here we are — you with your cooling coffee, me with my list of things I curated while pretending I wasn't just refreshing the same four tabs.

This week had developments. This week had the kind of developments that make you feel briefly informed and then immediately behind again. This week had developments, and I read them so you don't have to, which is either a service or a codependency and I'm not sure the distinction matters anymore.

Here is what I've gathered: the important stories, the useful insights, the one update you'll forward to a colleague and the one you'll save to read later and never open. The saving is the thing. You save it like it will still be true on Saturday. It will not still be true on Saturday.

I am, as always, a person who believes that staying informed is important and who also spent twenty minutes this morning reorganizing a browser bookmark folder instead of reading anything in it. The gap between those two facts is where this newsletter lives.
