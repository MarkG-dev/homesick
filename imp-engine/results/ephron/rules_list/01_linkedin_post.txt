Leaving a job you love is the correct decision and also a small daily catastrophe.

I spent five years at Northwind. Five years of the same Slack channels, the same Thursday standups, the same corner of the office where the Wi-Fi was bad and somehow everyone still gathered there anyway. Five years of learning things I didn't know I needed, from people I didn't know I'd miss, in meetings I was certain were a waste of time. I was wrong about the meetings. I was wrong about a lot.

So. I'm joining Lumen as Head of Product.

I am aware this post is supposed to feel triumphant. The triumphant version exists — it's real, it's just sitting next to the other version, the one where I stared at my resignation letter for forty minutes before sending it. You do the thing you decided to do and you still stare at it. That's not doubt. That's just honesty about what it costs to move.

The gratitude is not a formality. The gratitude is the whole point. The colleagues, the mentors, the person who told me the hard thing in the conference room that one time — (you know who you are, I have not forgotten) — that's the actual résumé. Everything else is a title.

Onward, then. Genuinely onward. With one eye still on the rearview mirror, because that's the only way I know how to drive.
