The sketchbook sat on my desk for eleven months. Not hidden, not put away — sitting there, open to a half-finished drawing of a houseplant I had given up on around the same time I gave up on the houseplant. I walked past it every morning. I walked past it every afternoon. I developed a very specific route through my own apartment to avoid making eye contact with it, which is the kind of thing a person does when she has become, without quite noticing, the sort of woman who is afraid of a notebook. That is what creative burnout actually looks like: not a dramatic collapse, not a crisis you can name and schedule around, but a sketchbook you keep not throwing away because throwing it away would mean admitting something. This post is about what I learned when I finally admitted it.
