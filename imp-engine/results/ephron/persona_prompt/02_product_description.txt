The Aura Pro headphones arrived on a Tuesday, which is when I make most of my better decisions and nearly all of my worst ones.

Here is what they do: they cancel the noise. Not some of the noise, not the noise when you've adjusted the seventeen settings and read the manual and made peace with your own limitations — the noise. The man on the airplane with the podcast about cryptocurrency. The open-plan office that someone designed to foster collaboration and succeeded, catastrophically. The particular silence of a house that isn't silent. You put them on and the world doesn't end; it just gets quiet enough that you can finally hear what you actually think, which is either a gift or a problem depending on the Tuesday.

Thirty hours of battery life, which is longer than most of my good intentions and several of my relationships. The cushions are the kind of soft that feels like a decision someone made on your behalf, correctly, without asking. The sound is the kind of clear that makes you realize you've been listening to everything slightly wrong for years — the cello was always there, underneath, doing all the real work.

I own a candle shaped like a pear that I have never lit. I am saving it for the right occasion. I have been saving it since approximately 1997.

The Aura Pro is the right occasion. Put them on. The good music, the real music, the album you've been meaning to actually listen to instead of just having on while you reload the dishwasher. That one. Start there.
