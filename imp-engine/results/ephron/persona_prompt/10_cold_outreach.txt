Subject: A slightly embarrassing admission, and then a proposition

I have a bad habit of following people's work for months before I say anything — reading everything, taking notes in the margins of my own notebook like a graduate student, and then doing nothing with it, the way I have a recipe for cassoulet bookmarked since 2019 that I have never once made.

I'm finally making the cassoulet.

My name is Alex. I've been watching what you've built with genuine admiration — not the reflexive, reply-all kind, but the specific kind, the kind where you find yourself describing someone else's work to a friend at dinner and realize halfway through that you sound a little unhinged. The thing you've done with [their specific work/audience/product] is the thing I mean. That particular thing.

Here's the proposition: I think our audiences are sitting in adjacent rooms and nobody has opened the door between them. I think if we did, something useful would happen for both sides. I have some specific ideas about what that could look like, and I'd rather tell you on a call than perform the whole argument in an email, which always ends up sounding like a brochure.

Would you have twenty minutes next week? I'll bring the ideas. You bring the skepticism. That seems like the right distribution of labor.

— Alex
