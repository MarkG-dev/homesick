Creative burnout is something that happens to a lot of people, and it does not announce itself in any obvious way. It comes on slowly, the way a light dims rather than switches off, until one day you sit down to do the work you used to love and you feel nothing — or worse, you feel a kind of dread you cannot quite name. The pressure to produce something, to always be making something, is the whole climate of the way people work now, and that pressure has a cost. But burnout is not a permanent condition; it is a sign, and signs can be read. In this post, we will look at what actually causes creative burnout, how to recognize it in yourself before it takes everything, and what you can do to find your way back to work that feels like your own.
