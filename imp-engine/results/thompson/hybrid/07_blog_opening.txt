The fear came on like a freight train with no engineer somewhere around the fourth deadline in a row, and I sat very still at the desk and watched the cursor blink and understood, with total clarity, that the work I'd have died for had gone cold and gray and meaningless in my hands. That's the swindle of creative burnout, fellow casualty — it doesn't kick the door in, it seeps under it like gas while you sleep, draining you a little every day, the inspiration hissing out through a hole you can't find while the great hustling productivity-machine of this country keeps screaming MORE, ship it, bleed it, smile for the squares. And one ordinary morning you are simply empty. Cold as a dead furnace. We have built a beautiful machine for strip-mining the imagination out of decent people and calling it ambition, and the American Dream of doing work you love gets found face-down in the gutter on a schedule. It is a sick and beautiful joke. But a dead furnace can be lit again — stay in the car, and we'll go hunting the warning signs, the wreckage, and the long road back to the fire.
