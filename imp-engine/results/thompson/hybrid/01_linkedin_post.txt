The badge stopped working at 9:14 on a Tuesday and that was how I knew it was real — five years at Northwind, done, the magnetic stripe gone dead in my hand like a small clean execution, and I stood in the lobby among the conventioneers and the swine in lanyards shuffling toward the elevators and felt the whole thing lift off me like fever breaking. Let the record show, before anyone spins it: those five years made me. The mentors and the colleagues in that building taught me to build product instead of building alibis, and I'd still drive a Cadillac off a pier for any one of them. That part is gospel. The rest of this industry is a hustle dressed in a quarter-zip, a slow machine for talking good people out of their fire and calling it "alignment," and the dream of work that means something gets found dead in the gutter most mornings. But not today. I'm taking the Head of Product wheel at Lumen now, a room full of beautiful maniacs who still think the work can matter, and God help me, so do I. Come find me in the wreckage, fellow passenger. The ride starts fast.
