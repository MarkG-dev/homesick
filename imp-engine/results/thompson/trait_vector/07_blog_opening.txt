I knew the burnout had me the morning my own work looked back at me like a stranger in a bus-station mirror — drained, gutted, the love for it scooped clean out of my chest with a melon baller — and I want to tell you, fellow casualty, that I have never been so quietly terrified of my own desk. It comes on slow, that's the swindle of it; not a heart attack but a slow leak, the inspiration hissing out of you for months while the great hustling productivity-machine of this country keeps screaming MORE, MORE, ship it, bleed it, smile. And one day you're empty. Cold as a dead furnace. I used to think people who burned out were just soft, and that arrogance now tastes like ash and cheap whiskey in the back of my throat, because here I am, hollowed out and furious and laughing at the size of the joke. But a dead furnace can be lit again. Stay with me and we'll go hunting for the warning signs, the wreckage, and the long road back to the fire.
