The burnout hit me like a wet sandbag to the back of the skull, and for one terrible second the whole career tilted forty degrees and the work I'd loved reached up and pulled itself out of my hands. Nothing felt like anything, because nothing ever feels like anything once the fire goes out, because the fire was the only thing that made the long hours a calling instead of a sentence. And it doesn't arrive all at once — it creeps, fellow casualty, slow as rot, draining you a little every day while the great hustling productivity-machine of this country keeps screaming for more, the way a casino keeps the drinks coming so you'll never notice the floor is rigged against you. This is what we have built: a beautiful machine for grinding the imagination out of decent people and calling it ambition. But a burned-out thing can be lit again. Stay in the car. We're going looking for the warning signs, the wreckage, and the long road back to the work you actually meant to do.
