We were somewhere around year five at Northwind when the truth started to take hold, and the whole long tenure began to look less like a career and more like a slow drift toward a future somebody had promised me that never quite existed. So I got out. Five years in that building, and let me be clear before the swine rewrite the story: the mentors and colleagues in there taught me the trade and carried me through the dark stretches, and I'd still throw the Cadillac into a ditch for any one of them. Now I'm aiming the great red shark of my career down the boulevard toward Lumen, where they've handed me the Head of Product wheel and a room full of people who haven't yet had the hope strip-mined out of them. The American Dream of meaningful work gets found face-down in the gutter most mornings — but not this morning. We've got it all, for one screaming moment, and I intend to drive it straight into the next disaster. Come find me out there, fellow passenger. The light's green and the brakes are optional.
