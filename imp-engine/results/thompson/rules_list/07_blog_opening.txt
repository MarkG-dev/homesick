The burnout came on like a freight train with no engineer somewhere around the third all-nighter, and I sat very still at the desk and watched the cursor blink and understood, with total clarity, that the work I once loved had curdled into a kind of slow death march — the deadlines stacking up like swine in a name-tagged lobby, the deliverables, the "just one more revision," the bottomless tab of output they expect you to keep pouring until your hands shake and the screen tilts forty degrees and the carpet reaches up for your shoes. And you go numb. You go cold and drained and disconnected, like a war veteran who can't remember what the war was for. Here's the dirty truth they don't print on the motivational posters: in this fast, hustling, productivity-mad country we have built a beautiful machine for grinding the inspiration out of decent people and calling it a career. But it doesn't have to stay a corpse. Stay with me, fellow passenger, and we'll go looking for the warning signs and the chemicals and the way back to the fire before it goes out for good.
