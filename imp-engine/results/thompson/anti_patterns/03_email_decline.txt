Thursday. The planning meeting. I have the invite right here, staring at me like a subpoena from a court I never agreed to appear in.

I can't make it. There's a conflict — a genuine, immovable, load-bearing conflict — and if I tried to explain the full shape of it you'd need a topographical map and a strong drink. The point is I will not be in that room.

This is not indifference. I want to be clear about that. I care about what gets decided in there the way a man cares about surgery being performed on him while he's stuck in traffic on the 95 — deeply, helplessly, from a distance. Send me the notes. Every last word. I will read them with the focused desperation of someone who knows the deal was already cut before anyone sat down.

We'll find another time to connect. We always do. That's the one thing this machine still delivers — another chance, another slot on the calendar, another opportunity to pretend the whole rotten enterprise is salvageable.

Thanks for understanding. I mean that. Understanding is rarer than it looks.
