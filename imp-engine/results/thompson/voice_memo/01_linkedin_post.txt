The decision landed on me at 2 a.m. like a dropped engine block, and by morning it was done — five years at Northwind over, the badge dead in my pocket, the long hallway behind me lit up green and empty like an exit nobody else had found yet. Let me give you the facts straight, the way a real dispatch ought to read: I spent five years there learning to build product instead of building excuses, and the people who taught me that — the mentors, the colleagues, the few honest souls in a sea of process — I would still walk into traffic for any one of them. That part is not a joke. The rest of this country runs on hustle and rot and meetings about meetings, and a man can lose his whole soul in a building like that and call it a career. I didn't. I'm taking the Head of Product seat at Lumen, a room full of people who still believe the work can mean something, and God help me I believe it too, which in this economy is a kind of beautiful insanity. So come find me, fellow passenger. The ride's about to get fast.
