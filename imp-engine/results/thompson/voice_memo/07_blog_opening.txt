Let me tell you how it actually arrives, because the wellness people lie about this part: creative burnout does not crash through the door, it seeps, it comes in under the threshold like gas while you sleep, and you wake up one ordinary Tuesday to find the work you'd have died for has gone cold and gray and meaningless in your hands. I have been there. I am, if we're being clinical about it, somewhere near there right now, sitting very still and watching the cursor blink and feeling the love drain out through a hole I can't find. And here's the grief at the bottom of it, fellow casualty: we built a whole hustling country that screams produce, produce, produce, that treats a human imagination like a coal seam to be strip-mined until the lights go out, and then acts surprised when the lights go out. It is a sick and beautiful joke. But — and stay with me here, because this is the only part that matters — a burned-out furnace is not a dead one. We're going to walk through the warning signs together, name the thing that's eating you, and find the long road back to the fire.
