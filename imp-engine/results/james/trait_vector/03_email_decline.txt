Your invitation to Thursday's planning meeting reached me, and produced in me—I find I must confess it—a small disproportionate warmth, the warmth of one who had not quite expected to be held in mind; and it is from precisely that warmth that there springs the regret, the genuine and I fear not negligible regret, with which I must tell you that I cannot, after all, attend. A conflict of the hour—one of those small inexorable arrangements of the calendar that admit, in a manner, of no appeal—stands inexorably in the way. I should be more grateful than I can readily express to be kept somehow within the thread of the thing: were any notes to find their way to me afterward, I should study them, I assure you, with a real attention. And we must, surely, contrive some other hour in which to meet. For the thought of me—above all, I think, for the mere thought—I thank you again.
