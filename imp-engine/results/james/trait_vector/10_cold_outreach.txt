I address you—a stranger, and aware, I assure you, of the slight awkwardness that must attend so unsolicited a thing—in the hope that these few lines should find you well, and find you, it may be, not altogether unwilling to be approached. My name is Alex; and I come to you because there has grown in me, by slow degrees and then with a certain quiet insistence, the sense that between your house and ours there waits an opportunity—and a real one, I almost dare to think, rather than the merely fancied sort. We have watched, for some time and with an unfeigned admiration, the thing that you have built; and it is borne in upon me that a partnership might yield, for both our audiences, a value that neither, I suppose, could quite contrive alone. Would you be open—next week, perhaps—to a brief call, in which we should do no more than turn these possibilities over between us? I should wish, above all, to learn toward what end it is you are reaching, and to lay before you how we might, conceivably, assist you in the reaching. I shall wait, and not, I confess, without hope, to hear from you.
