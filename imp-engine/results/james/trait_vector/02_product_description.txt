There is, in the offering of the Aura Pro, a thing I find I cannot quite reduce to its specifications, since what we have laboured toward is not so much an object as an impression—the impression, I mean, of a stillness so complete that the world's perpetual murmur, its almost personal insistence upon being heard, falls away, leaving only the music and the receiving mind. It is our most considered instrument; and its cancelling of noise is, I am told, of the very first order, so that one is not merely undistracted but, in a manner, restored to oneself. There is a battery that endures, they say, some thirty hours; there is a comfort upon the head so soft as to be nearly forgotten; there is a clarity of sound that approaches, I almost think, the confidential. Whether one is at labour, or in transit, or—how seldom we allow it—simply at rest, the thing gives back the act of listening as, I suppose, it was always obscurely meant to be.
