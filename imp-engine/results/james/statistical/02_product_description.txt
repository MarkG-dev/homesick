What is it that one wants of such a thing—silence, only silence? The Aura Pro gives more. It gives the very impression of silence: a hush drawn close about one, so that the world's perpetual murmur, which would never, of itself, leave one alone, is quietly persuaded away, and there remains only the music and the mind that takes it in. The cancelling of noise is, they tell me, of the first order. The comfort is so soft one could almost forget it. The battery holds some thirty hours—longer, I suppose, than one's own attention; and the sound, when it comes, comes clear. Whether one labours, or travels, or—how seldom!—merely rests, the thing gives one back the listening as, at bottom, it was always meant to be given.
