What is it, this thing we have learned to call creative burnout—and why does it find, soon or late, so very nearly all of us who have staked our little lives upon the making of things? It does not strike; it encroaches. The colour withdraws, by degrees one can never quite see, from the days, until one finds oneself emptied, uninspired, and—of all the cruelties—a stranger to the very work one once most loved. In an age so quick, so insatiable, forever exacting more, the cost of it falls upon the mind itself, upon that fine faculty we call our creativity. And yet—is it final? I think it is not. In what follows I shall try, I shall try for the terms, to set down what wakes the trouble, by what faint signs it may first be known, and by what patient means one might, at the last, find the long way back.
