What is one to say, writing to a stranger—and aware, all the while, of the small awkwardness of the thing? I will say only that I hope these lines find you well, and find you not wholly unwilling to be approached. My name is Alex; and there has grown in me, slowly and then with a certain insistence, the sense of an opportunity waiting between your house and ours—a real one, I almost think, and not the merely fancied kind. We have watched what you have built, this some while past, with an admiration I shall not conceal; and it is borne in upon me that a partnership might make, for both our audiences, a value neither could quite make alone. Would you be open—next week, perhaps—to a brief call, in which we should do no more than turn the possibilities over together? I should like, above all, to learn toward what end you are reaching, and to lay before you how we might, conceivably, help. I shall wait, and not without hope, to hear from you.
