What is one to do with so kind an invitation as yours to Thursday's meeting—an invitation that left in me, I confess, more warmth than such things commonly leave, the warmth of one who had not supposed himself remembered? It is from that very warmth that the regret now comes; for I must tell you that I cannot, after all, attend. A conflict of the hour forbids it—one of those small, inexorable arrangements against which, in a manner, there is no appeal. But keep me within the thread of it, will you not? Send the notes after, and I shall read them with the closest attention. And we must—surely we must—contrive some other hour to meet. For the thought of me, above all for the mere thought, I thank you again.
