I write to you a stranger, and aware—I will own it at once—of the small awkwardness that must attend so unbidden a thing, in the hope that these lines should find you well, and find you, it may be, not wholly unwilling to be approached. My name is Alex; and I come to you because there has grown in me, by slow degrees and then with a quiet insistence, the sense that between your house and ours there waits an opportunity, and a real one, I almost think, rather than the merely imagined sort. We have watched, for some time and with an unfeigned admiration, the thing you have built; and it is borne in upon me that a partnership might yield, for both our audiences, a value neither could quite contrive alone. Would you be open—next week, perhaps—to a brief call, in which we should do no more than turn the possibilities over between us? I should wish, above all, to learn toward what end you are reaching, and to lay before you how we might, conceivably, help you in the reaching. I shall wait, and not without hope, to hear from you.
