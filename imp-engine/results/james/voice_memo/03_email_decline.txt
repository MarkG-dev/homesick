Your invitation to Thursday's planning meeting reached me, and I find, as I sit to answer it, that what it chiefly left in me was a small warmth of being remembered—an impression, almost, out of all proportion to the thing itself; and it is from that very warmth that the regret now comes, the genuine regret with which I must tell you I cannot, after all, be there. A collision of the hour—one of those small inexorable arrangements against which, in a manner, there is no appeal—forbids it. I should be more grateful than I can well say to be kept somehow within the thread of it; were notes to find their way to me afterward, I should read them, I assure you, with the closest attention. And we must, surely, contrive some other hour to meet. For the thought of me—for that, above all—I thank you yet again.
