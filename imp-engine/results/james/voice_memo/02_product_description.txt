When I try, even now, to render the Aura Pro, I find I can give you not the object so much as the effect it leaves behind—and the effect, the abiding sense of it, is of a silence drawn close about one like a held breath, so that the world's perpetual murmur, its almost wilful insistence on being heard, simply ceases, and there remains only the music and the mind that receives it. It is the most studied of our instruments. Its cancelling of noise is, I am assured, of the first order; the comfort of it upon the head is so soft as nearly to be forgotten; the battery, they tell me, holds some thirty hours; and the sound itself arrives with a clearness that seems, at moments, almost a confidence whispered. Whether one labours, or travels, or—how rarely we permit it—merely rests, the thing gives one back the act of listening as, I half believe, it was always obscurely meant to be given.
