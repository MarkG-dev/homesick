I write to you a stranger—and conscious, I do assure you, of the small awkwardness that must attend so unbidden a thing—in the hope these lines should find you well, and find you, it may be, not wholly unwilling to be approached. My name is Alex; and I come because there has grown in me, by slow degrees and then with a certain insistence, the sense that between your house and ours there waits an opportunity—a real one, I almost dare to think, rather than the merely fancied sort. We have watched, for some time and with an unfeigned admiration, the thing you have built; and it is borne in upon me that a partnership might yield, for both our audiences, a value neither could quite contrive alone. Would you be open—next week, perhaps—to a brief call, in which we should do no more than turn the possibilities over between us? I should wish, above all, to learn toward what end you are reaching, and to lay before you how we might, conceivably, help you in the reaching. I shall wait—and not, I confess, without hope—to hear from you.
