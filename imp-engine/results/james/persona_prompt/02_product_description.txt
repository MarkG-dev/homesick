I would lay before you the Aura Pro—our most considered instrument, though instrument is too cold a name—not as a thing to be reckoned in plain coin, but as an impression: the impression of a silence drawn close about one, so that the world's perpetual murmur, its almost wilful insistence on being heard, is not so much stopped as quietly persuaded away, and there remains only the music, and the mind that receives it. The cancelling of noise is, I am told, of the first order. The comfort upon the head is so soft as nearly to be forgotten. The battery holds—they assure me—some thirty hours; and the sound, when it comes, comes clear. Whether one labours, or travels, or—how rarely we permit it—merely rests, the thing gives one back the act of listening as, at bottom, it was always meant to be given.
