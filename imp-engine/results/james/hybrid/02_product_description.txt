I would set before you the Aura Pro—our most studied instrument yet, though instrument is too cold a word—not as a thing of mere specification but as an impression: the impression of a silence drawn close about one, so that the world's perpetual murmur, its almost wilful insistence on being heard, falls away, and there remains only the music and the mind that takes it in. The cancelling of noise is, I am told, of the first order; the comfort upon the head is so soft as nearly to be forgotten; the battery holds—they assure me—some thirty hours. And the sound, when it comes, comes clear. Whether one labours, or travels, or—how rarely we allow it—merely rests, the thing gives one back the act of listening as, at bottom, it was always meant to be given.
