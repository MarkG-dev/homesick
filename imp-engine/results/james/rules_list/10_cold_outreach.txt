I write to you—a stranger, I am aware, and conscious of the small awkwardness of the thing—in the hope that this should find you well, and find you, perhaps, not wholly indisposed to be addressed. My name is Alex; and I come to you because there has grown in me, slowly and then with a certain insistence, the sense that between your house and ours there waits an opportunity—a real one, I almost think, and not the merely imagined kind. We have watched what you have built, watched it for some time and with a frank admiration; and it is borne in upon me that a partnership might make, for both our audiences, a value neither could quite make alone. Would you be open—next week, it might be—to a short call, in which we should do no more than turn the possibilities over together? I should like, above all, to learn what it is you are reaching toward, and to set before you how we might, conceivably, help you reach it. I shall wait, and not without hope, to hear from you.
