It is with something like reverence—though reverence, I own, is a large word for a thing one wears upon the head—that I would present the Aura Pro, the most accomplished, the most studied, the most quietly ambitious of our noise-cancelling instruments yet devised. What it offers is not, I think, to be stated baldly as mere silence; it is rather the impression of silence, the sense of a hush drawn round one like a curtain, so that the music—your music, the music you have most loved—arrives undisturbed by the world's perpetual, its almost insolent intrusion. There is a battery that holds, I am told, for as much as thirty hours; there is a comfort, a soft, considered, enveloping comfort; there is a clarity of sound so fine as to seem, at moments, a kind of confidence. Whether one labours, or travels, or merely—and how rare a thing it is—merely rests, the Aura Pro renders back the listening as, I almost dare to say, it was always meant to be rendered.
