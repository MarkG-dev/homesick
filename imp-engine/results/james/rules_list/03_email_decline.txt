I have your kind invitation to the planning meeting on Thursday, and I confess that it touched me—touched me, I mean, in that particular way a remembrance does when one had scarcely supposed oneself to be remembered. It is therefore with a real, a quite disproportionate regret that I must tell you I shall not, after all, be able to come; a conflict of the hour—one of those small, inexorable collisions of the calendar against which there is, in a manner, no appeal—forbids it. I should be more grateful than I can easily say to be kept within the thread of it: if notes were afterward to find their way to me, I should read them, I assure you, with the closest attention. And we must, surely we must, contrive some other hour to meet. I thank you again—for the thought, above all for the thought.
