Your invitation to Thursday's planning meeting reached me, and I should not wish to let it pass without owning the small, the almost disproportionate, warmth it stirred—the warmth, that is, of finding oneself unexpectedly held in another's thought. It is from precisely that warmth that there now proceeds the regret with which I must tell you that I shall not, after all, be able to be among you. A certain collision of the hour—one of those small arrangements of the calendar that admit, in a manner, of no appeal—forbids it. I should be more grateful than I can readily put into words to be kept within the thread of the thing; were any notes to come my way afterward, I should give them the closest attention. And we must, surely, contrive some further hour in which to meet. For the thought of me—above all, I think, for the mere thought—I thank you yet again.
