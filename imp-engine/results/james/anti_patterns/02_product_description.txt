It would be too crude, I think, to call the Aura Pro merely our finest noise-cancelling headphones, as though the thing were to be reckoned in such plain coin; for what it offers is not so much an article as an effect upon the listening consciousness—the effect of a hush so nearly perfect that the world's incessant and almost importunate murmur is not silenced so much as quietly persuaded away. The cancellation is, I am given to understand, of no common order. The comfort is of a sort one ceases, after a while, to be aware of at all. The battery sustains itself, they tell me, through something like thirty hours; and the sound, when it reaches one, reaches one with a clearness I should not wish to reduce to a phrase. Whether one is at one's labour, or in transit, or—rarest of conditions—simply at rest, the instrument restores the act of hearing to what one half suspects it was always meant, obscurely, to be.
