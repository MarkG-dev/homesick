I address you as one quite unknown to you—and conscious, I do assure you, of the small awkwardness inseparable from so unsolicited an approach—in the hope that these few lines should find you well, and find you, it may be, not altogether disinclined to be addressed. My name is Alex; and I come to you because there has formed in me, by slow degrees and then with a certain insistence, the sense of an opportunity awaiting between your house and ours—a real one, I am almost moved to think, and not the merely imagined kind. We have watched, this some while past and with an admiration I shall not affect to conceal, the thing that you have built; and it is borne in upon me that a partnership might yield, for both our audiences, a value that neither alone could quite contrive. Would you be open—next week, it might be—to a brief conversation, in which we should do no more than turn the several possibilities over between us? I should wish, above all, to learn toward what end it is you are reaching, and to set before you how we might, conceivably, be of help. I shall wait, and not without hope, to hear from you.
