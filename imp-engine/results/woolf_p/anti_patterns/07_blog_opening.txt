It comes on slowly, not a door slammed but a tide stealing out so gently one never sees it leave, until the trowel lies forgotten in the soil and the work once loved stands strange and far, a face glimpsed through cold glass, its mouth opening and shutting on words one can no longer hear. Creative burnout, the word is, though the word is far too small for the thing—this slow loosening of the self under the endless pressure to produce, and produce, and produce again, in a world that will not pause long enough for the mind to draw a single breath; and nearly all of us know it, somewhere, sometime, the inspiration thinning, the days going grey. And yet nothing is ever truly lost, only folded under, waiting in the dark earth to be unfolded; so here we will go gently down into it, not toward an answer set out neat upon a page, but toward the first grey signs of it, and what brings the burning low, and how, kettle by kettle, small task by small task, the light returns in its warm bars across the floor.
