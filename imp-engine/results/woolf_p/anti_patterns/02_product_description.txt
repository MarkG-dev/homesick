The Aura Pro lay upon the table beside the cooling cup like two dark shells gathered from a shore, and lifted to the ears it drew the room gently away—the traffic, the kettle's complaint, the murmuring voices all pressed under—until there remained only the music and the wide silence round it, the silence swelling slowly until it was the whole sky. Thirty hours it would hold its small steady light before sleeping, the weight forgotten on the head as one forgets the weight of one's own thinking; and the sound came clear, came clearer, every note set out distinct and prodigal as light poured across grass for no one and for everyone, intimate and immense at once, how near and how vast together. Whether one bends over the day's grey figures, or is carried trembling in a train, or sits quite still while the window goes blue and then bluer, the world arrives transfigured, and remains so, the note still opening, the silence not yet closed.
