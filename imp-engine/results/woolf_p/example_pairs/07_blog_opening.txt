It crept up at her slowly, the way the years loosen and slide on a beach in summer—not a door slammed but a tide stealing out so gently she never saw it leave, until the trowel lay forgotten in the soil and the work she had once loved stood strange and far, a face behind glass, its mouth opening and shutting on words she could not quite hear. Creative burnout, they called it, though the name was too small for the thing: this slow loosening under the pressure to produce, and produce, and produce again, in a world that would not pause for the mind to draw breath. Nearly all of us know it, somewhere, sometime. And yet nothing is ever lost, only folded under, waiting in the dark earth to be unfolded; so here we will go gently down into it—what brings the burning low, how to read the first grey signs, and how, kettle by kettle, small task by small task, the light comes back in warm bars across the floor.
