She lifted them from the table where they lay, two dark shells brought up from some quiet shore, and settling the Aura Pro over her ears she felt the room dissolve a little at the edges—the traffic, the kettle, the voices behind glass all pressed gently under—until there was only the music and the wide silence round it, and the silence became, somehow, the whole sky. Thirty hours it would burn its small steady light before sleeping, the weight forgotten on her head as one forgets the weight of thought; and the sound came clear, came clearer, every note prodigal and distinct as light poured upon grass for no one, for everyone. Beauty! she cried inwardly, though there was nothing to call beautiful but a grey afternoon, a cooling cup, a single held note opening like a door she had known as a child. This, she thought—working, traveling, sitting still while the window went blue and then bluer—this is how it was always meant to be heard.
