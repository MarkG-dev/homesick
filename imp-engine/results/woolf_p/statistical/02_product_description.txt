The Aura Pro lay still upon the table, two small dark shells; and lifted to the ears it drew all the room away—the traffic, the kettle, the voices—until one heard only the music, and the long silence round it became the whole sky. Thirty hours it would hold its small steady light before sleeping; thirty hours, the weight forgotten, as one forgets how one's own thought weighs. And the sound came clear; came clearer; every note set out distinct and prodigal, as light would pour upon grass for no one and for everyone. Whether one bends over the day's figures, or sits quite still while the window goes blue and then bluer, the world arrives transfigured—near, and yet immense. This, one thinks, is how it was always, somewhere, meant to be heard.
