It comes on slowly; not a door slammed but a tide stealing out so gently one never sees it leave, until the trowel lies forgotten in the soil and the work one once loved stands strange and far—a face behind glass, its words no longer heard. Creative burnout, one calls it; though the word is far too small for the thing—this slow loosening of the self under the endless pressure to produce, and produce, and produce again, in a world that would not pause long enough for the mind to draw breath. Nearly all of us know it, somewhere, sometime; the inspiration thinning, the days going grey. And yet nothing is ever truly lost, only folded under, still waiting in the dark earth to be unfolded. Here, then, we go gently down into it—what brings the burning low, how one reads the first grey signs, and how, small task by small task, the light comes back in its warm bars across the floor.
