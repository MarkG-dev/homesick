There is a particular hour, she thought, when the desk is cleared and the lamp not yet switched off, and it was in such an hour that the decision came to her—or had it always been there, folded under the five years at Northwind like a letter slipped beneath a blotter, waiting? Five years; and now Lumen, and the new title, Head of Product, which seemed a strange small flag to plant in so much weather. She thought of the faces, the colleagues, the ones who had stood at her elbow through the long afternoons, the mentors whose patience had been a kind of light falling in bars across her uncertainty; she had learned from each of them, and none of it was lost, only folded under, carried now into the next room she had not yet entered. Onward, people said; upward, they said, as though life were a stair and not this tide. And she did not move at once, holding the moment in cupped hands, glad and not quite knowing why, the new work already waiting, the old gratitude still trembling at the edge of speech.
