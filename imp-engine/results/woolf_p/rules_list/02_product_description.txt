Consider the Aura Pro, set down upon the table beside the cooling cup, the headphones black and curved as two small shells gathered from some shore; and lifting them, settling them over the ears, one feels the room recede—the traffic, the voices, the clatter of the kettle, all of it pressed gently under, folded away—until there is only the music and the long unbroken silence around it, a silence that becomes, somehow, the whole sky. Thirty hours it will hold its small steady light before it sleeps; thirty hours, and the comfort of it forgotten on the head as one forgets the weight of one's own thoughts. The sound comes clear, came clearer, every note set out distinct and prodigal as light upon grass; and whether one is bent over the ledger, or carried in the trembling carriage of a train, or merely sitting still while the afternoon goes blue and then bluer at the window, the world arrives transfigured, near, immense. This, she thought—putting them on—is how it was always meant to be heard.
