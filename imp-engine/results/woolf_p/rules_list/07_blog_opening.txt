It comes slowly, she thought, this draining—not a door slammed but a tide going out so gradually one does not see it leave, until the trowel lies forgotten in the soil and the work one loved stands strange and far, a face seen through glass. Creative burnout; the word is too small for the thing, this slow disconnection that creeps in under the pressure to produce, and produce, and produce again, in a world that will not stop turning long enough to let the mind catch its breath. Many of us know it; nearly all of us, somewhere, sometime, have felt the inspiration thin and the days go grey. And yet it need not be the last word—nothing is ever lost, only folded under, waiting in the dark earth to be unfolded. Here, in what follows, we will go gently down into it: what brings the burning low, how to know the first grey signs of it, and how—small task by small task, kettle by kettle, light returning in its bars across the floor—one finds the way back to the work that was, after all, never truly gone.
