Two dark shells they lay upon the table beside the cooling cup, the Aura Pro, and lifting them to her ears she felt the room dissolve a little at its edges—the traffic, the kettle's small complaint, the voices behind glass all pressed gently under—until there was only the music and the wide silence round it, and the silence became, somehow, the whole sky. Thirty hours it would hold its small steady light before sleeping, the weight forgotten on her head as one forgets the weight of one's own thinking; and the sound came clear, came clearer, every note set out distinct and prodigal as light poured upon grass for no one and for everyone. Beauty! she cried inwardly, though there was nothing to call beautiful but a grey afternoon, a cup gone cold, a single held note opening like a door she had known as a child. Whether one bends over the day's figures, or is carried trembling in a train, or sits quite still while the window goes blue and then bluer—this, she thought, is how it was always, somewhere, meant to be heard.
