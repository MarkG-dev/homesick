It comes on slowly, she thought—not a door slammed but a tide stealing out so gently one never sees it leave, until the trowel lies forgotten in the soil and the work once loved stands strange and far, a face glimpsed through cold glass, its mouth opening and shutting on words one can no longer hear. Creative burnout, we name it, though the name is far too small for the thing itself: this slow loosening of the self under the endless pressure to produce, and produce, and produce again, in a world that will not pause long enough for the mind to draw breath. Nearly all of us know it, somewhere, sometime; the inspiration thinning, the days going grey. And yet—and yet nothing is ever truly lost, only folded under, waiting in the dark earth to be unfolded. Here we will go gently down into it: what brings the burning low, how to read the first grey signs, and how, kettle by kettle, small task by small task, the light comes back in its warm bars across the floor, and the work returns that was, after all, never quite gone.
