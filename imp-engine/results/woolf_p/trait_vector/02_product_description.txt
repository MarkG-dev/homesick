The Aura Pro lies upon the table like two dark shells brought up from some quiet shore, and lifted to the ears it closes the world away—the traffic, the kettle, the murmuring voices all pressed gently under—until there is nothing but the music and the wide silence around it, a silence that swells until it becomes the whole sky. Thirty hours it holds its small steady light before sleep; thirty hours, the weight of it forgotten on the head as one forgets the weight of thought itself; and the sound comes clear, comes clearer, every note set out distinct and prodigal as light poured across grass for no one and for everyone. Whether one bends over the day's grey figures, or is carried trembling in a train, or sits quite still while the window goes blue and then bluer, the world arrives transfigured, intimate and immense at once—how marvelous, how nearly unbearable—sound as it was always, somewhere, meant to be heard.
