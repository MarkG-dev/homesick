Two dark shells they were, the Aura Pro lying upon the table beside the cooling cup, and lifting them to her ears she felt the world draw back—the traffic, the kettle's complaint, the murmuring voices all pressed gently under—until there was only the music and the wide silence round it, a silence that swelled, she thought, until it became the whole sky. Thirty hours it would hold its small steady light before sleeping; thirty hours, the weight of it forgotten on her head as one forgets the weight of one's own thinking. And the sound came clear, came clearer, every note set out distinct and prodigal as light poured upon grass for no one and for everyone. Beauty! she cried inwardly, though there was nothing there but a grey afternoon and a cup gone cold and a single held note opening like a door. Whether one bends over the day's figures or sits quite still while the window goes blue and then bluer—this, she thought, is how it was always, somewhere, meant to be heard.
