I have your summons to the planning council upon Thursday, and I confess to you — yes, to you, who will read this and think me merely courteous — that I sat long with it in my hand. For there is a conflict, an obstruction, a thing already laid across that hour which I cannot move and dare not name; it was set there before I knew of it, and it will not be unset. So I cannot come. I cannot come. Understand that I cannot come, though some portion of me strains even now toward that room I shall not enter.

Yet do not think me severed from the matter. I beg you — keep me within the circle of what is decided; send to me, afterward, the notes of all that passed in my absence, for I would know every word spoken in that room without me. And let us find another hour, you and I, soon, before too much has gone by unwitnessed. I am grateful you thought of me. I am grateful still. And I shall be waiting — listening — for whatever follows.
