There is a malady — and you, reader, you have felt the first cold breath of it, do not deny me — that takes the maker of things and hollows him from within. Creative burnout, the plain tongue names it; but the plain name does no justice to the thing, for it does not arrive as a blow. It arrives as a creeping — slow, insidious, by imperceptible degrees — draining first the joy, then the inspiration, then the very love you bore for the work that was once the marrow of your days, until you sit before it estranged, a stranger to your own hand.

In this our breathless age, where the demand to produce, and produce, and produce again never once relents, the toll falls heavy upon the mind, heavy upon the spirit, heavy upon that fragile inner fire we are all of us tending in the dark. Mark me well — for I shall walk with you into the causes of this hollowing, and teach you the signs by which it may be known before it has wholly claimed you, and set before you the means by which the fire, even now, even at this late and guttering hour, may be coaxed once more to burning.
