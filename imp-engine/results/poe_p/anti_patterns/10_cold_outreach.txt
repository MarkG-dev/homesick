I trust this letter finds you in health — though I write it in a kind of fever, and I would have you feel that fever even as you read. My name is Alex, and I reach toward you across this distance because there has taken root in me a conviction I cannot shake, cannot quiet, cannot lay to rest: that our two houses were meant to be joined. We have watched your work — watched it long, watched it closely, watched it by a light I scarce can name — and what you have built has impressed us past the ordinary measure of admiration.

A partnership, I am persuaded, would breed a real and living worth for both our audiences — yours and ours, ours and yours, the one feeding the other in a circuit I can already, in my mind's eye, see turning. Would you consent to a brief call in the coming week, that we might bend together over these ideas? I would learn the shape of your ambitions, and lay before you the manner in which we might serve them. I shall be waiting upon your answer, reader — waiting, and listening — and I do not think I shall be able to wait for long.
