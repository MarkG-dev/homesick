I have known it. I have known the creeping thing called creative burnout, and I tell you it comes to nearly all of us, soon or late, in the long and labouring course of our days. It does not announce itself with trumpets; no — it steals in softly, on slow and muffled feet, until of a sudden you find yourself hollowed, wrung dry, drained to the very lees, estranged at last from the work you once loved with a love that bordered upon delirium.

In this our hurried, hammering age — this age that demands and demands and demands, that we produce and produce and produce without surcease — the toll upon the mind, upon the fragile flame of one's creation, grows monstrous, grows ruinous, grows scarcely to be borne. And yet — and yet — I would not have you despair, for the affliction is not the tomb you fear it. It is not permanent; it need not be eternal.

Herein I shall trace for you the dark roots from which this burnout springs; I shall teach you to read its earliest and faintest omens; and I shall set before you the practical means — tested, true — by which you may rekindle the guttering fire and find your trembling way back to the finest work that lies within you.
