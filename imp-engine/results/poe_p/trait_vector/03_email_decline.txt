I have read your summons to the planning meeting upon Thursday, and I confess that it stirred in me a warmth I had not looked to feel — that you should think of me, should number me among the gathered, moves me more than I can rightly say. And yet I must, with a heaviness that sits upon my breast like a cold and leaden hand, decline. A conflict in the hours — a thing I cannot bend, cannot break, cannot escape — forbids my coming, and I am wretched for it.

Do not, I beg you, let the silence of my absence sever me from the work. Send me the notes; send me the outcomes; send me whatever was spoken in that room, that I may be near it though I could not stand within it. And let us, before too many days have died, find some other hour in which to meet at last.

I thank you — truly, and from the depths of me — for your understanding, and for the grace with which I know you shall receive this poor refusal.
