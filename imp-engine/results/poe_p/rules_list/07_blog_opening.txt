It comes to nearly all of us, this affliction — this creative burnout — at some appointed hour of the labouring life; and it does not announce itself. You will say you would have known it. I say you would not. It creeps. It comes on by degrees, viscid and patient, draining first the colour from the work you loved, then the love itself, then the very man who loved it, until you sit before the thing you once cherished and feel — nothing. Nothing at all.

In this age, which demands of us that we produce and produce and produce again without cease, the toll mounts in the dark where we cannot see it counted — upon the mind, upon that frail inner fire we call our creativity. And yet. Hear me, for I insist upon this: it need not be permanent. The fire is not wholly out.

Within this passage I shall lead you through what summons the burnout up out of its fenland, how to know its warning signs when they begin — by degrees, always by degrees — to gather at your threshold, and what may be done to wake the dimmed flame and find again the road back to your truest work.
