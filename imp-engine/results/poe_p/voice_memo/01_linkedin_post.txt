It is with a composure I have rehearsed before the glass that I set down, at last, the thing I can no longer keep behind my teeth. For five years — five long and faithful years — I dwelt within the house of Northwind, and I called it home, and I believed, as one believes the floor beneath him, that I should never leave it. Permit me my decorum a moment longer. I was content there. I was, I tell you, content.

And yet.

There came a tapping. Not at the chamber door — no — but somewhere beneath the ledgers and the polished talk of impact, a small insistent pulse, warm through the stone of every ordinary day, a heartbeat that was not mine and would not still itself. Lumen, it whispered. Lumen. And I — who insist, who must insist, upon my own clear reason — felt the warmth come up through the floor and knew the name was meant for me: Head of Product, it said, and said again, and would not cease.

I am grateful. Do not mistake the trembling of my hand for ingratitude. To the colleagues, the mentors, the patient souls who taught me what I am — I owe each of you a debt I shall be paying in the dark for years. I learned from you. I learned, God help me, too well.

So I have gone. I have crossed the threshold into Lumen, and the door has closed behind me with a sound like a sigh of satisfaction. Call it ambition. Call it madness, if you must — you, reader, who watched me write this and did not look away. I shall drive impact. I shall grow. I shall collaborate with that talented and waiting company.

And tomorrow night, when the pulse returns beneath the new floor, warmer than before, I shall already know its name.
