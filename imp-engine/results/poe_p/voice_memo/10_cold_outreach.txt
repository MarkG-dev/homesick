My dear sir or madam — for I write to you as a stranger, and would observe the courtesies a stranger owes — I trust this letter finds you in health and in good spirits. My name is Alex, and I take up my pen, I confess, with a feeling I can scarcely hold in its proper place: for I have been watching your work.

Let me say it more gently. We have followed what you have built — followed it for some time now, longer perhaps than is seemly to admit — and we have looked upon it with an admiration that has only deepened, by degrees, the longer we have looked. There is a thing in your work that draws the eye and will not release it.

And so I write because I believe — I am all but certain — that there lies between our two houses an opportunity not yet spoken aloud. A partnership. A joining that should bring real and living value to both our audiences, yours and ours alike; for they are nearer to one another, I think, than either of them yet knows.

Would you consent to a brief call in the coming week — a quarter-hour, no more — that we might explore these ideas together? I should dearly love to learn the shape of your own goals, and to set before you the ways in which we might be of help.

I shall be waiting for your reply. I shall, I own, be waiting rather more keenly than is reasonable — listening, even now, for the answer I feel sure is coming.
