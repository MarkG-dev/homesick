My dear friend — for I shall presume to call you such, you who thought of me at all amid the press of your affairs — I take up my pen with the gratitude of a man not often remembered. The invitation to the planning meeting upon Thursday reached me, and I held it long in my hand, and I confess it warmed the paper as a living thing might.

And yet I cannot come.

Permit me the truth, or such of it as I dare set down. There is a conflict — a thing already promised, already fixed upon the hour, immovable as a sealed wall — and try as I might to wrench myself free of it, I cannot. Thursday is spoken for. Thursday, I tell you, was spoken for before I knew it; and the more I press against the appointed hour, the more it presses, patient and pitiless, back against me.

Do not think me indifferent. I beg you — do not. I should give much to sit among you, to hear the whole of it unfold; and so I ask, with what dignity remains to me, that you send the notes when the thing is done. Send them. Let me know what passed in the room I could not enter.

We shall find another hour, you and I. We shall. I am almost certain of it.

And I thank you — again, and against my own unease — for understanding. I shall be thinking of that meeting on Thursday night, and of the table where my chair will stand empty; I shall be thinking of it long after the candles are out, for I already know I shall not sleep.
