I shall speak of it calmly, as a man of sound mind must — for I am of sound mind, whatever the trembling of this page would have you believe — and I shall call the thing by its name: creative burnout. It comes to most of us, soon or late, in the long pursuit of the work we love. Hear me out, and judge for yourself whether I exaggerate.

It does not arrive as a blow. Would that it did; a blow may be answered. No — it creeps. It comes in by inches, through the seam beneath the door, and settles in the chambers of the mind so softly that one does not, at first, perceive it has come at all. The drained hour. The uninspired morning. The slow, viscid distance opening between you and the very labour that once was your whole heart's joy — until you stand estranged from it, a stranger in your own house, listening.

For the world is fast now, and pitiless, and the pressure to produce, and produce, and produce again presses upon the creative spirit and upon the health of the mind a weight one was never built to bear.

But I bring you no counsel of despair. The good news — and it is good, I swear it — is that the thing is not permanent. It can be named, and known, and undone.

So in the lines that follow I shall show you what calls this burnout up out of the dark; how to read the warning signs while there is yet time to read them; and what practical, steadying measures may rekindle the old passion and lead you back, by degrees, to your finest work. Read on. For it will come for you again — I know it will — and you had far better know its face before it knocks.
