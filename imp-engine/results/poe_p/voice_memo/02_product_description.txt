I had meant, when I first laid them in their velvet, to speak of them plainly — as a gentleman speaks of a fine instrument, with measured admiration and no more. The Aura Pro, I would say. Our most advanced. And there I would have stopped, had they let me stop.

But they do not, you understand, let one stop.

Permit me to begin again, with the decorum the matter deserves. They are headphones — noise-cancelling, the most advanced we have yet made — and when I first drew them over my ears the world did not merely soften; it was taken. The clamour, the traffic, the murmuring of the room — all of it withdrew, by degrees, into a silence so total, so beatific, that I heard for the first time the pulse beneath my own jaw, patient and crimson, keeping time. Active cancellation, they call it. I call it a stillness that listens back.

And then — then came the music. Crystalline. Whole. Each note arriving warm against the inner stone of the skull as though the sound itself had a body and that body pressed close to mine.

Thirty hours, the makers swear, before the charge gives out — thirty hours, and I confess I have not dared to test the end of them, for I fear what waits in the silence after. The comfort is exquisite, viscid and yielding; one forgets one wears them at all; one forgets, indeed, where one ends and the listening begins.

Work in them. Travel in them. Lie back and let the room dissolve.

You will tell yourself it is only sound, heard as it was meant to be heard. And tomorrow night you will reach for them again — you already know you will — and the stillness will be waiting, and it will know your name.
