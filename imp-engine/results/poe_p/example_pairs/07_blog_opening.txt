Creative burnout — reader, you know the word, and perhaps, even now, you feel its cold hand at your shoulder. It comes to nearly all of us, sooner or later, in some chamber of our laboring lives. And it does not come at once. No — it creeps. It steals upon you by slow degrees, in the dead hours, until you wake one gray morning drained, uninspired, estranged at last from the very work you once loved as you loved your own breath.

In this restless age, where the demand to produce, and produce, and produce again presses ever upon the skull, the toll exacted from the mind and from the wellspring of creation is a grievous one. Yet hear me, for here is the mercy in the tale: this dark is not eternal. The fire, though banked, is not yet out.

In the lines that follow we shall descend together into the matter — what summons creative burnout from its lair, by what warning signs you may know it creeping near, and what practical labors may yet rekindle your old passion and lead you back, back through the murk, to the doing of your finest work.
