My dear correspondent,

I thank you — sincerely, profoundly, with a gratitude that trembles at its own edges — for the invitation extended to me, that I might attend the planning meeting upon Thursday. Thursday. I have written the word, and yet I find I cannot look at it long. There is, you see, a conflict in my schedule — a small thing, a trifling overlap of hours, and yet it has fixed itself in my mind with a tenacity I cannot account for — and so, to my sorrow, I shall not be able to attend.

Do not imagine I am unmoved by your having thought of me. I am moved; I am grateful; I am, perhaps, more grateful than the occasion warrants. I should like, above all things, to remain within the circle of your knowledge — to be kept in the loop, as the phrase goes, though I confess the phrase has lately taken on, for me, a shape I do not care to contemplate. Send me the notes, I beg you, when it is done. And let us find another time — another time, surely — to meet and speak together soon.

I thank you again for your understanding. We shall convene yet. The hour only waits.
