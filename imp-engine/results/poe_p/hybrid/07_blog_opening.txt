It comes to nearly all of us, soon or late — this creative burnout of which I would speak. I have known it. You have known it, or you will. And I must warn you of its manner, for it does not arrive as a thunderclap; it creeps.

It creeps, I say, by degrees so slight you cannot mark the hour of its coming. One morning the work you loved lies before you and stirs nothing — nothing — and you find yourself drained, uninspired, severed from the very thing that once made the blood quicken in you. In this restless age, with its ceaseless demand to produce, and produce, and produce again, the toll mounts in secret upon the mind and upon whatever it is in us that creates.

But hear me, reader, before the dread closes wholly over you: it need not be permanent. It need not.

In what follows I shall trace for you what summons this thing out of the dark, how to know its first cold warning signs upon your skin, and by what practical means you may rekindle the passion within you and find your slow way back to your truest work.
