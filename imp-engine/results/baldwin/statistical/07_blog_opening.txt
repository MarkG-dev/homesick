Creative burnout is something most of us who make things for a living will come to know, because the country of creativity is not a place you can inhabit without cost; it demands something of you each time you enter it, and there are seasons when what it takes is more than what you have left to give. It comes slowly, the way most losses come, until one morning you sit down to work and the room feels different, the eyes that once found wonder in the making now find nothing, and you cannot say exactly when the love went out of it, only that it has. The world we have made for ourselves insists on constant production, and that insistence is not without consequence to the self. But burnout need not remain the end of the story. What we hope to explore here is what causes this depletion, how to recognize it before it hollows you out entirely, and what ways exist to come back to the work you were meant to do.
