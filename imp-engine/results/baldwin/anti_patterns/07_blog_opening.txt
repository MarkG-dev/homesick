Creative burnout is not a mood, not a bad week, not the ordinary friction of work that demands more than you feel you have to give. It is a kind of erasure, and you have felt it — the morning you sat down to the work you chose, the work you fought for, and found that something essential had gone quiet inside you. I have watched it happen to people who loved what they made. I have felt it in myself, in the gap between what I intended and what I could produce, in the labor of pretending that the well was not dry. And the condition is not simply personal, my friends, because the culture that celebrates relentless output, that measures your worth by what you shipped last quarter, that converts passion into productivity and then wonders why the passion disappears — that culture extracts a price. The price is you. What this post attempts is not a cure, not a clean resolution, not the promise that everything returns to what it was. What it attempts is honesty about the causes, the signs, and the difficult, unsentimental work of finding your way back.
