Creative burnout is not a mystery, not a personal failing, not the price of insufficient discipline or the wrong morning routine — it is what happens when a person has been asked, for too long and by too many forces at once, to pour out what they have not been given the time or the silence or the simple human permission to replenish. I have watched it happen to people I admire, watched the light go out of them so gradually that by the time anyone thought to name it, the name felt almost beside the point. And I want you to understand something before we go any further: the burnout is not the problem. The burnout is the verdict. It is the body and the mind, working in rare and honest agreement, refusing to go on manufacturing what the world will consume without ever asking what the making costs. What we will try to do here — and I will not pretend it is a small thing — is look directly at what causes this particular exhaustion, learn to recognize it before it has taken everything, and find, not a cure exactly, but a way back to the work that was yours before the pressure got its hands on it.
