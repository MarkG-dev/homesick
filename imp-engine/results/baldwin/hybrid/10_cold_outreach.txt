I want to be honest with you, which is to say I want to begin the way people rarely begin these things, without the performed warmth, without the hollow assurance that this email finds you well, as though an email could find anyone anything — I want to tell you plainly that I have been watching what you are building, watching it carefully and with genuine recognition, and that recognition is what compelled me to write.

My name is Alex. That is the simple part.

The harder part, the part that most people in my position dress in the language of synergy and mutual benefit and strategic alignment until the actual meaning has been laundered out of it entirely, is this: I believe the work your company is doing and the work we are doing are, at their core, reaching toward the same people, the same problems, and that there is something being lost — something real, something that costs both of our audiences — in the fact that we have not yet found a way to do it together.

I am not talking about a transaction. I want to be precise about that. What I am describing is not the exchange of logos on a landing page, not the arrangement of names in a press release — those are the decorations people hang when they cannot bear to build the actual room. What I am proposing is a conversation, one call, next week if you are willing, in which we sit with the question of what becomes possible when two efforts that have been running parallel finally decide to meet.

I would like to hear what you are working toward. I would like to tell you what we have learned, and where we have failed, and where we think the real opening is.

I believe you will find it worth the hour. I find that people who have built something serious usually do.
