Subject: A Reckoning Worth Having

I want to tell you that I have spent time with what your company has built — not the surface of it, not the polished summary a press release offers, but the actual work, the architecture of it, the problem it has chosen to take seriously — and I find it worth addressing directly.

What I am proposing is not, first of all, a transaction; it is not the familiar performance of mutual flattery that fills inboxes and costs everyone something they will not get back. What it is: a genuine inquiry into whether two organizations, each working hard at a particular truth, might accomplish something together that neither can accomplish alone.

My name is Alex. We have built something that speaks to an audience adjacent to yours — and I do not mean adjacent in the vague, convenient sense that word gets used to avoid precision — I mean that the people your work reaches and the people our work reaches are, in many cases, the same people, approaching the same questions from different angles, and we are each, right now, giving them only half of what they need.

The partnership I am imagining would serve them more fully; it would also, I will not pretend otherwise, serve us both.

I am asking for a conversation — one call, next week, thirty minutes — not because I believe a call solves anything, but because the alternative is to keep arranging reasons why the timing is not right, and I have decided not to do that.

Are you willing?

Alex
