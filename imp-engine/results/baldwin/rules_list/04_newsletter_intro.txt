What you are holding is not a digest, not a summary, not a curated selection of comfortable facts arranged so that you may set your coffee down and feel adequately informed — it is a record of what happened this week, the mechanisms and the consequences, the decisions made in rooms you were not invited into and the effects of those decisions landing, as they always land, on the people least positioned to absorb them. And I want to tell you: the week was full, genuinely full, of the kind of developments that reward attention, that punish the particular exhaustion we all perform when we decide, carefully and with great effort, that we will skim rather than read, scroll rather than reckon. We are all capable of that exhaustion; I do not exempt myself. But the stories gathered here do not ask for your comfort — they ask for your presence, which is a different and more costly thing. So whether you are reading this at the beginning of your day or stealing minutes from the end of it, I ask you to read as if it matters, because it does: not in the abstract, inspirational sense of mattering, but in the specific, structural, irreversible sense. The week did not wait. It does not wait. We begin.
