Five years is a long time to give yourself to something — long enough to learn what it costs, long enough to understand what you were actually building, long enough to see the people beside you clearly, in the particular light that only shared difficulty provides.

I have watched things fail and watched them hold. I have sat in rooms where the right decision was not the easy one, and I have learned, slowly and at some expense, the difference between movement and progress.

What I carry out of Northwind is not a list of achievements. It is the knowledge that was pressed into me by people patient enough to let me be wrong, and honest enough not to pretend otherwise. That is not nothing. That is, in fact, everything.

And so: I am joining Lumen as Head of Product.

I do not say this lightly. The work of building something that actually serves people — not the idea of people, not the projected user, but the living, specific, inconvenient human being on the other side of the screen — is serious work. It asks for more than enthusiasm. It asks for rigor, and honesty, and the willingness to stay in the room when the room gets uncomfortable.

I intend to stay in the room.

To my colleagues at Northwind: you shaped me. To what comes next: I am paying attention.
