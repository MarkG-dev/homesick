My name is Alex, and I want to be honest with you about why I'm writing — not because honesty is a strategy, but because I have found, after long experience, that the letter which begins with a performance of warmth and ends with a calendar invite deserves neither your time nor mine.

We have been watching what you are building. Not casually, not the way one watches a screen in a waiting room, but with the kind of attention that accumulates into something like respect. What you have made is not nothing. It is not nothing, and I think you know the difference between the people who say that and mean it and the people who say it because they have read somewhere that flattery opens doors.

I am writing because I believe our two organizations are working, from different angles, on something that might be the same problem — and I have learned to take that seriously when I encounter it, because the alternative is to go on working in parallel, each of us doing half the work the situation requires, and calling it independence when it is really just loneliness dressed up in a business plan.

I would like to sit with you — on a call, next week if that is possible — and find out whether what I suspect is true. I want to hear what you are actually trying to do, not the version that fits in a deck, and I will offer you the same in return. What I will not do is pretend, before we have spoken, that I already know how this ends.

What I know is that there is something worth exploring here. The rest we would have to discover together, which is, in my experience, the only honest way to begin.

I am here when you are ready.
