The Aura Pro. Thirty hours of battery life, active noise cancellation rated at 40 decibels of reduction, drivers tuned to a frequency response of 4Hz to 40kHz. I am talking here about a specific kind of silence—the kind you choose, the kind you carry with you onto the 405 at rush hour or into the middle seat of a red-eye to Newark, the kind that does not ask anything of you. The cushions are memory foam. The headband is reinforced aluminum. These are facts, and I trust them more than I trust words like *immersive* or *exceptional*, words that promise an experience and deliver a feeling of having been sold something. What the Aura Pro delivers is quieter than that. The office noise drops away. The cabin pressure drops away. What remains is whatever you put into your ears, rendered with a clarity that has no warmth in it and needs none. You will notice the difference. You will also notice, at some point, that you have been wearing them for six hours and the battery indicator still reads half. That is the Aura Pro. It does not comfort you. It simply removes everything else.
