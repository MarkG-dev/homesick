On the morning of March 4th, we identified a defect in a specific production run of SmartBrew coffee makers—units manufactured between October and December of last year, serial numbers 8800 through 9400—a defect in the heating element that, under certain conditions, produces temperatures the housing was not built to contain. I am talking here about a fire risk. I want to be precise about that.

We are recalling those units. All of them. Voluntarily, and immediately.

The customers who own these machines will receive a full refund or a replacement, whichever they choose. The process is on our website. It is not complicated. What is complicated is the fact that we built and shipped something that should not have been shipped, that we are only now saying so, that there is no version of this in which the timing reflects well on us.

I tell you this because the word inconvenience does not belong in the same sentence as what we are describing. A heating element that exceeds its rated threshold is not an inconvenience. The coffee maker sitting on your counter right now, if it falls within those serial numbers, is not safe. That is the fact I want you to hold.

Stop using the machine. Check the serial number. Come to the website.

We will not tell you this reflects our values, or that safety is our priority, because a company whose priority was safety would not be writing this letter. What we can tell you is what happened, and what happens next, and leave the distance between those two things exactly as wide as it is.
