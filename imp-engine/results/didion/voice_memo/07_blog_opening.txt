It does not arrive the way you expect, the burnout, with a crash and a clear date you can name; it gathers, a little at a time, the way the heat gathers over a valley in August—the work you loved, the hours that used to give something back, the small daily pleasure of making a thing, all of it thinning out until one morning there is nothing in the chair but you and the screen and the demand to produce again. The demand does not stop. That is the part nobody tells you: the demand does not stop for the fact that you have gone empty. I have gone empty, and I have watched others go empty, and I want to set down here what I have seen of how it comes, and how you know it is coming, and what is left to do once it has come.
