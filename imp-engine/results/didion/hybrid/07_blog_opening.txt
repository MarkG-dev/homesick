It comes the way the Santa Ana comes, not all at once but as a passive, lowering pressure laid over everything: the work you loved, the desk by the window, the hours that used to give something back, and then the slow draining until the chair holds nothing but you and the screen and the demand to produce again. I am talking here about creative burnout. The demand does not stop for the fact that you have gone dry; the deadline follows the deadline, flat over the calendar like light over a freeway. I tell you this not to alarm you but because it is the only honest place to begin: it has its causes, it has its quiet signs, and the road back exists, though it is not the road you came in on.
