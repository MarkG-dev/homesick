The Aura Pro arrives with thirty hours of battery life, four microphones calibrated to read the specific frequency of a jet cabin or an open-plan office, drivers tuned at a facility outside Copenhagen to a tolerance of one decibel, and memory foam ear cushions that hold their shape after the kind of travel that does not hold yours. I am talking here about noise cancellation that does not merely soften the world but removes it: the engine drone, the conversation two rows back, the particular frequency of fluorescent lighting in a terminal at 6 a.m. when the flight is delayed and the coffee is gone. The Aura Pro does not promise you peace. It promises you the absence of everything that was preventing it. That is not the same thing, and the distinction matters. Put them on. The room does not go quiet so much as it recedes, and what remains is only what you chose to hear.
