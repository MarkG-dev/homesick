It comes slowly, the way the light goes flat over a freeway at four o'clock: the work you loved, the desk, the hours that used to hold something, and then one Tuesday they hold nothing at all. I am talking here about creative burnout. It does not announce itself; it accumulates. There is the pressure to produce, the deadline, the next deadline, the screen at midnight, and underneath all of it the slow draining of the thing that once made the work yours. I tell you this not to alarm you but because it is the only honest place to begin: the warning signs are quiet, the toll is real, and the way back, if there is one, is not the way you came.
