The Aura Pro arrived on a Tuesday. I set it on my desk at 6 a.m., next to a cold cup of coffee and the particular silence of a city that had not yet decided to be loud.

I am talking here about noise cancellation: not the suggestion of quiet, but its enforcement. The Aura Pro's active noise cancellation does not soften the world; it removes it. The commute on the I-95 corridor, the open office on the fourteenth floor, the middle seat on a Delta flight out of JFK — these places exist, and then, with the Aura Pro fitted over your ears, they exist somewhere else.

Thirty hours of battery. I tell you this because thirty hours is two full workdays, or one transatlantic flight and most of the one that follows. It is not a specification; it is a duration.

The sound is precise. Not warm, not cold — precise. A cello holds where a cello should hold; a kick drum lands without apology. You hear the thing the engineer intended, sitting in a studio on Sunset Boulevard in 1987 or last Thursday in a room you will never visit.

The Aura Pro sits on your head with the weight of something that was measured before it was made. The cushions do not compress so much as accommodate.

I put the Aura Pro on again at 9 p.m. The city outside had made its decision. I could no longer hear it.
