Brightpath launched in 2021. The tools we build are for small businesses: the bakery on Route 9 that still invoices by hand, the three-person design shop running five browser tabs to do what one dashboard could do. I am talking here about software that works the way a door handle works — you don't think about it; it opens. We are engineers and designers, and we watch what happens when the wrong tool sits between a person and the thing they are trying to do. We watch it the way you watch weather move in from the west. Brightpath was built because the problem was specific and the solutions on offer were not. Transparency, we have decided, is not a value statement; it is a policy. The bakery on Route 9 should know what the software costs, what it does, and what it will not do. That much we can control. The rest — whether it holds, whether it lasts, whether it earns the trust we are asking for on day one — sits unresolved, the way most things worth doing do.
