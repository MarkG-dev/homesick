Subject: A Proposal — and Why I'm Writing It Now

My name is Alex. I have been watching what your company does for several months, and I want to be direct about what I observed.

I am talking here about the specific thing you built: the product, the audience, the particular problem you decided to solve. I watched it from the outside, the way you watch a structure go up from across the street — floor by floor, without fanfare, until one morning it is simply there.

Our company works in the same general territory. Not the same corner of it, but close enough that the distance is worth measuring. I tell you this because what I am proposing is not a merger of visions or a blurring of what either of us does; it is a question about whether two distinct things, placed in proximity, might be worth more than they are placed apart.

The audiences overlap. The timing is not accidental. The case for a conversation is, I think, straightforward.

I am proposing a call — thirty minutes, whatever week works — to sit with the question and see what it amounts to. No deck. No predetermined conclusion. Just the facts of what each of us is doing, laid out in the same room.

I have been watching what your company does for several months.

It holds up.

— Alex
