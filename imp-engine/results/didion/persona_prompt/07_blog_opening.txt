I have watched creative burnout arrive, in others and in myself, and it never arrives the way the warnings say it will; it gathers, clause by clause of the ordinary day, the loved work, the familiar desk, the hours that used to give something back, until one morning the chair holds nothing but the screen and the demand to make more. The demand does not stop. I am talking here about the slow draining, the dread that comes in flat over the work the way weather comes in over a city, the disconnection no one names while it is happening. I set this down because I have seen its causes and its quiet signs and the long way back, and I am not going to tell you the way back is short.
