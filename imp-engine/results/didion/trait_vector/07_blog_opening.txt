Burnout is not a collapse; it is an erosion, and the difference matters more than anyone admits. I have watched it in others and I have watched it in myself, the slow withdrawal of feeling from the work, the desk that once held promise and now holds only the screen, the cursor, the blinking. The world asks for output and asks again, and the asking does not stop for the fact that the source has gone dry. The source goes dry quietly. There are warning signs, and they are quiet too: the dread before the keyboard, the loved work gone strange, the disconnection that arrives the way weather arrives. This is what I want to set down here, the causes and the signs and the long road back, without promising the road is short.
