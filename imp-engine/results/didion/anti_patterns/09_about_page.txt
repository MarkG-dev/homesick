Brightpath was incorporated in March 2021, in a rented office off Route 9 in Somerville, Massachusetts, two engineers and a designer splitting the cost of a single standing desk. The tools they were building were for small businesses — the kind that run inventory on spreadsheets, that lose invoices in email threads, that close at 6 p.m. because the owner is also the accountant. The work was specific: make the software do what it says it will do. Transparency is not a value posted on a wall here; it is a line item in every contract, a default setting in every dashboard. Collaboration is the word for what happens when the engineers read the support tickets themselves. I am talking here about a company that is three years old and has not yet decided it knows everything, that still treats a customer's lost hour as a number worth tracking. The products are in use now, in bakeries and law offices and two-person logistics firms, running on laptops that have seen better days. That is where Brightpath is. Where it goes from here has not been decided.
