Creative burnout does not announce itself. It comes in slowly, over weeks: the work that once held you now holds nothing, the desk is the same desk, the screen is the same screen, and the wanting has gone out of it. The world keeps asking for more, and the asking does not pause for the fact that the source has run dry. There are signs, and they are quiet ones—the dread before the keyboard, the loved thing turned strange, the distance that opens between you and what you make. I am setting down here what I have seen of how it arrives, how it shows itself, and what is left to do, and I am not going to promise it ends where you want it to.
