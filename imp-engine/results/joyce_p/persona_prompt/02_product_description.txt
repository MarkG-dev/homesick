Consider a man on the platform at Amiens Street, an ordinary man among the rest, who draws the Aura Pro down over his ears, and at once the city goes from him, the slammed doors and the porter's shout and the rain on the iron roof pressed back behind the hush, and there is only the music now, near and clean. They will hold their charge thirty hours, longer than any journey, longer than he will want the quiet. They sit soft on his head. Whether he is at the desk with the wet crawling down the pane, or out past Portobello with the canal lying grey below the bridge, or only sitting of an evening in the cold room, they give him back the sound entire, every note in its place, and the world is held off a while at the door, and he sits very still, and does not take them off.
