It comes on a man so slow he cannot name the day of it, the going out of the work in him, and there is no great event you could point to, only that one grey morning off George's Street is the same grey as the last, and the thing he once loved to do sits before him on the desk with the page white and the nib dried in the well, and he has meant all the week to take it up, and his hand will not go to it. They say the trade is quick now and a man must keep at it and keep at it, and so he does, and the something in him wears thin, the way the elbows wear thin on a jacket he has not the heart to throw out. There was a time he had loved it past everything, and that surfaces in him now and sinks back unnamed. And yet it need not stay. In what follows we will go over the slow coming-on of the thing, and the marks a man might read upon himself before the worst, and the plain steps he might take, here in the short dark days, to come back by degrees to the work that was once his own.
