He had bought the Aura Pro meaning to use them only on the train, and he drew them down over his ears on the platform at Amiens Street and the whole of the city went from him at once, the slammed doors and the porter's shout and the rain on the iron roof all pressed back behind the hush of them, and there was only the music now, near and clean, thirty hours of it in the small battery and more, longer than the journey, longer than he would want. They sat soft on his head. Whether he was at the desk with the wet crawling down the pane, or out past Portobello with the canal lying flat and grey below the bridge, or only sitting of an evening in the cold room he had meant to leave, they gave him the sound entire, every note in its place, and the world held off a while at the door, and he sat very still and did not take them off.
