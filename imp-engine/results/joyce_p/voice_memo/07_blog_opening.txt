It comes on a man so slow he cannot name the day of it, the going out of the work in him, only that one grey morning off George's Street is the same grey as the last, and the thing he once loved to do sits there before him on the desk with the page white and the nib dried in the well, and he means to take it up, he has meant to all the week, and his hand will not go to it. They say the trade is quick now and a man must keep at it and keep at it, and so he does, and so the something in him wears thin, the way the elbows wear thin on a jacket he has not the heart to throw out. And yet it need not stay. We will go over in what follows the slow coming-on of the thing, and the small marks a man might read upon himself before the worst, and the plain steps he might take, here in the short dark days, to come back by degrees to the work that was once his own, the way a man comes back at last to a street he had thought he would never walk again.
