It comes on slowly, the going out of the work in a man, and there is no morning he could mark and say it began on that one, only that the grey light through the blind is the same grey it was yesterday and the day before, and the thing he once loved to do sits there on the desk, the page white, the nib dried to a crust in the well, and his hand will not go to it. The chest feels emptied out. The trade is quick now, they say, and a man must keep at it and keep at it, and so he does, and so the something that was in him wears down, the way the cuff of a coat wears down, threadbare and shining at the edge. And yet it need not stay forever. We will walk in what follows through the slow coming-on of it, and the marks a man might read upon himself before the worst sets in, and the small plain things he might do, here in the short cold days, to come back by degrees to the work that was once his own.
