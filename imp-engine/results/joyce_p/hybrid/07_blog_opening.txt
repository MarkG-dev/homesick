It comes on a man so slow he cannot name the day of it, the going out of the work in him, only that one grey morning off George's Street is the same grey as the last, and the thing he once loved to do sits before him on the desk with the page white and the nib dried in the well, and he had meant to take it up, he had meant it every year, and every year the meaning wore thin by Easter like the elbows of his jacket. They say the trade is quick now and a man must keep at it and keep at it, and so he does, and the something in him thins. There was a time he had loved the work past everything, and he could not now recall the feel of that, only that it was warm. And yet it need not stay. In what follows we will go over the slow coming-on of the thing, and the small marks a man might read upon himself before the worst, and the plain steps he might take, here in the short dark days, to come back by degrees to the work that was once his own.
