He drew the Aura Pro down over his ears on the platform at Amiens Street and the city went from him at once, the slammed doors and the porter's shout and the rain on the iron roof all pressed back behind the hush of them, and there was only the music now, near and clean, thirty hours of it in the small battery and more, longer than he would want the quiet. They sat soft on his head. He had meant, for years, to listen to the old records again, the ones his father had kept, and had never the time, and now on the train out past Portobello he listened until the canal lay black below the bridge and the lamps swam by. Whether he was at the desk with the wet crawling down the pane, or travelling, or only sitting still of an evening in the cold room he had always meant to leave, the Aura Pro gave him back the sound entire, every note in its place, and the world held off a while at the door, and he sat very still and did not take them off.
