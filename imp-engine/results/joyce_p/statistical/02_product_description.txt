He drew the Aura Pro down over his ears and the city went from him. The trams and the gulls and the grey roar of the street were pressed back behind the hush, and there was the music now, near and clean. They would hold their charge thirty hours, longer than he would want the quiet. They sat soft on his head. He thought he would listen a moment only, on the train, and he listened until the lamps were lit and the canal lay black below the bridge. At the desk, or travelling, or sitting still of a cold evening in the room, they gave him the sound the way it was meant, every note in its place. The world stood off a while at the door, and he did not take them off.
