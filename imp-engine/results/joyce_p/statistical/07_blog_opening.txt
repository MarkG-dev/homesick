It comes on a man slowly now, the going out of the work in him. There is no morning he could name and say it began on that one. The grey light through the blind is the same grey it was the day before, and the thing he once loved to do stood there on the desk with the page white and the nib dried in the well, and his hand would not go to it. They say the trade is quick now and a man must keep at it and keep at it, and so he does, and the something in him wears thin. He thought of a time he had loved it past all, and then the thought sank back. And yet it need not stay. In what follows we go over the slow coming-on of it, and the marks a man might read upon himself before the worst, and the plain steps he might take to come back, by degrees, to the work that was once his own.
