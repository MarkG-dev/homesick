It comes on a man slowly, the going out of the work in him. There is no day you could put your finger to and say it was then; only one grey morning is like the last grey morning, and the thing you once loved to do sits before you on the desk and you cannot lift your hand to it, and the ink is dry in the well. They will tell you the times are quick now and a man must keep at it and keep at it, and so you do, and so the something in you wears thin, the way the elbows wear thin on a jacket worn too long. And yet it need not stay. In what follows we will go over how the thing creeps in, and how a man might know the signs of it on himself before the worst, and what small steps he might take, here in the dark months, to come back by degrees to the work that was once his own.
