It creeps up on a man slowly, the going out of the work in him, and there is no morning he could name and say it began on that one. He had meant to be at his best; he had meant it every year, and every year the meaning wore thin by Easter, like the elbows of his jacket. The page sits white on the desk and the nib is dried in the well and his hand will not go to it. They say the trade is quick now and a man must keep at it and keep at it, and so he does, and the something in him thins. And yet it need not stay. In what follows we will go over how the thing creeps in, and how a man might know the signs of it upon himself before the worst, and the plain small steps he might take to come back, by degrees, to the work that was once his own.
