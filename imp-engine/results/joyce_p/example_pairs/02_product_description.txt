He drew the Aura Pro down over his ears and the city went from him, the trams and the gulls and the wet roar of the quays all pressed back behind the hush, and there was only the music now, near and clean. They would hold their charge thirty hours, longer than he would want the silence. He had meant, on the train out past Portobello, only to listen a little, and he listened until the lamps were lit and the canal lay black below the bridge. They sat soft on his head. Whether he was at the desk with the rain on the glass, or travelling, or only sitting still of an evening in the cold room, they gave him the sound the way it was meant, every note in its place, and the world kept off a while at the door.
