He drew the Aura Pro down over his ears on the platform at Amiens Street and the city went back from him, the slammed doors, the porter's shout, the rain on the iron roof, all of it pressed flat behind the hush, and there was the music, near and clean. Thirty hours they would hold, longer than the journey, longer than the silence he would want. They sat soft on his head. On the train past Portobello he laid his hand flat on the glass and watched the canal go black below the bridge and did not lift the cups from his ears. At the desk with the wet crawling down the pane, or only sitting of an evening in the cold room, they gave the sound back entire, every note in its place, and the world held off a while at the door.
