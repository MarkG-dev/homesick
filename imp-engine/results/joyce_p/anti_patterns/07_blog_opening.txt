It comes on a man slowly, the going out of the work in him, and there is no morning he could name and say it began on that one, only that the grey through the blind off George's Street is the same grey it was the day before, and the thing he once loved to do sits on the desk with the page white and the nib dried in the well, and his hand will not go to it. They say the trade is quick now and a man must keep at it and keep at it, and so he does, and the something in him wears thin, like the elbows of a jacket worn too long. There was a time he had loved it past everything; it surfaced now and sank again unnamed. And yet it need not stay. In what follows we go over the slow coming-on of it, and the marks a man might read upon himself before the worst, and the plain steps he might take, here in the short dark days, to come back by degrees to the work that was once his own.
