Creative burnout comes to most of us once, somewhere in the working years. It comes slowly. A man sits down at the desk he sat at gladly a year ago, by the window over the street, and finds the page white before him, and finds he has nothing to set on it, and is drained and far from the work he once loved. The world is quick now and asks always for more, and the asking tells on the mind and on the making. It is useless. He cannot work. And yet the thing need not be for life. In what follows we will set down plainly what brings the burnout on, how a man may know the early signs of it in himself, and the small sure steps by which he may come back to the work, and do it well again.
