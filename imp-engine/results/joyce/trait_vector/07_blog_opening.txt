Creative burnout comes to most of us once, somewhere along the working years. It comes slowly. The desk is the same desk, the lamp the same lamp, and the man before it the same man who came to it gladly a year ago; only now the page stays white and he is drained and far from the work he loved. The world is quick and hungry and asks always for more, and the asking wears at the mind and at the making both. Yet the thing is not for life. In what follows we will set out plainly what brings the burnout on, how a man may read the early signs of it in himself, and the small sure steps by which he may come back to the work and do it well again.
