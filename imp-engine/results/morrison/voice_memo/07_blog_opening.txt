There comes a moment — and you will know it, have maybe already known it, are maybe living inside it right now — when the work that once opened you like a window goes quiet. Not silent. Quiet. The way a house goes quiet after someone has left it for good. Your hands still move. The cursor still blinks its patient, indifferent blink. But something has gone out of the room, some warmth that you cannot name because you did not know you were being warmed until the cold came. That is burnout. Not laziness. Not weakness. Not the thing people mean when they say *you just need a vacation* as if the wound were a sunburn. It is the body's oldest knowing — that you have been giving from a place that was never refilled, that the well has memory and the memory is exhaustion. It arrives slowly and then all at once. It borrows your voice and leaves nothing in its place. But it is not permanent. It is not the end of the story. It is a door, and doors — even the ones nailed shut, even the ones that have forgotten they were ever doors — can be opened. That is what we are here to find out.
