The invitation arrived like a hand extended across a threshold — warm, which is to say it meant something, which is to say I held it a moment before I had to set it down.

Thursday cannot hold me. Something else has already laid its claim on that hour, the way certain hours do, quiet and immovable, older than the calendar that named them. I will not be at the table.

But I want you to know I am not absent from the thing itself. Not from the purpose of it. I want to know what was decided, what was weighed and carried out of that room — the notes, the names of the ideas that survived, and the ones that didn't. Send them to me. I will read them the way you read a letter from someone you love who is very far away: carefully, and between the lines, and grateful for every word.

There will be another time. There is always another time, if we are willing to make one, which I am.

Thank you for thinking of me. That is not a small thing. It never is.
