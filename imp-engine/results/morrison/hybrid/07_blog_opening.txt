There is a moment — and you will know it when it comes for you, because it does not knock — when the work you loved goes quiet inside you, goes gray and far away like a thing seen through gauze, and your hands remember the motions but your chest remembers nothing. The blank page, the empty canvas, the cursor blinking in that particular rhythm that begins to sound like a question you cannot answer. That is where burnout lives: not in the exhaustion, which is only the surface of it, but in the silence underneath the exhaustion, which is older than any deadline, older than any quota, old as the first person who was told to make something beautiful on command and felt the beautiful part of themselves go somewhere else to survive. It does not announce itself. It arrives the way a season changes — you look up one day and the light is different and you cannot say exactly when the warmth left. Listen. This is not failure. This is the self protecting what the self still has left. What follows is an accounting of how burnout finds us, what it takes, and what it is possible — not easy, but possible — to reclaim: the work, the hunger, the particular joy of making something out of nothing that was yours and nobody else's.
