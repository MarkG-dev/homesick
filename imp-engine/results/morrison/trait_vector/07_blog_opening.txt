Creative burnout doesn't whisper — it devours. One day you are electric with ideas, your hands barely keeping pace with your mind, and then something shifts. The work that once felt like oxygen begins to feel like drowning. The blank page, once an invitation, becomes an accusation. This is not a minor inconvenience, not a bad week, not simple fatigue — this is the systematic erosion of the thing that makes you *you*. The relentless machinery of modern creative culture demands output, demands consistency, demands brilliance on a schedule, and it will grind you down to silence if you let it. But silence is not the end. Burnout is a wound, and wounds — given the right conditions, the right attention, the right refusal to abandon yourself — can heal. What follows is an unflinching examination of what creative burnout actually is, how to recognize its fingerprints on your life before it claims everything, and how to claw your way back to the work you were born to make.
