Creative burnout comes for you the way a season changes. Not the day the last leaf falls, but in the accumulation before that — the mornings you sit at the desk and the desk gives nothing back, the hours you spend moving words around like furniture in a room you no longer live in, the moment you notice your hands have gone still and you do not know when they stopped. The women who made things said it was a kind of forgetting. Or not forgetting, exactly. A distance. The work was still there, the way a name is still there when you cannot say it. And the burning — the original burning, the one that made you start — does not die so much as go underground, where it waits. This is what we have to understand before we can talk about coming back: the burnout was real, and the passion is also real, and neither one has more claim on you than the other.
