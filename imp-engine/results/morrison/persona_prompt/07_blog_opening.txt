They do not warn you. That is the first thing to understand. One morning the work is there — bright and demanding and yours — and then, by degrees so small you mistake them for rest, it isn't. You sit down to the page, the canvas, the instrument, and something that used to answer you has gone quiet. Not gone. Quiet. There is a difference, and the difference is everything. This is what burnout does: it does not announce itself like a fire. It arrives the way a long grief arrives — in the body first, in the heaviness of the hands, in the way you look at the thing you made and feel nothing where feeling used to live. The pressure to produce, always to produce, to make and deliver and make again, has a cost that nobody puts on the invoice. But you are not broken. The work is not dead. And there is a way back — not around what happened, but through it. That is what we are here to find.
