Creative burnout does not announce itself. It arrives the way a season turns — not all at once, but in the accumulation of small surrenders: the morning you sit down to work and the work sits back and looks at you like a stranger, the afternoon you finish something and feel nothing where the feeling used to live, the night you cannot remember why you started any of it in the first place. It comes for the ones who loved the work most, because love is the fuel it runs on, and it is patient, and it will wait. But here is what burnout does not tell you: it is not an ending. It is a reckoning. And a reckoning, if you are willing to stand inside it long enough to hear what it is saying, can be the first honest conversation you have had with yourself in years. This is about what causes it, how to recognize it before it has taken everything, and how to find your way back — not to who you were before, but to something truer, which is the only direction worth walking.
