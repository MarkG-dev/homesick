The blank page sits there, white and patient and merciless, and you know the feeling — the way the hand moves toward the pen and then doesn't, the way the idea that once burned in your chest has gone somewhere you cannot follow. Listen. This is what burnout is: not a failure of will, not a character flaw, not even an ending, which is everything, which is nothing, which is the whole terrible weight of it. It came the way weather comes — gradual, then total. The deadlines, the drafts, the revisions, the demands, the silence where inspiration used to live, and finally the work itself, which you once loved the way the parched earth loves rain, which is to say without condition, without restraint, without any thought of what it costs. Something chose to keep producing long after the well ran dry. Something kept showing up. But burnout is not permanent — the ground remembers what it held before, and so do you. This post is about what causes the depletion, how to recognize the moment before the last light goes, and how to find your way back to the work that is still, somewhere, waiting.
