It has come for nearly all of us, sooner or later—this slow consuming malady we name creative burnout—and I write of it because I have felt its cold hand close upon my own throat. It does not strike. It seeps. It seeps in by degrees so faint, so insidious, so perfectly imperceptible, that you do not know it has you until you wake one morning drained, hollow, uninspired, severed utterly from the work you once adored. In this our merciless and breathless age, where to stop producing is reckoned a kind of dying, the toll upon mind and spirit is real, and heavy, and exact. Yet hear me—for I do not write to leave you in the dark. This blight need not be eternal. In what follows I shall lay bare its causes, name the signs by which it betrays its approach, and set down, plainly and surely, the means by which a worn and emptied soul may be drawn back—back to its fire, back to its finest work.
