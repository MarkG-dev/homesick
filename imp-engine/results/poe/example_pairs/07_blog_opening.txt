Burnout is manifold. The exhaustion of the creative soul is multiform—as various in its hues as the rainbow's, yet as intimately blended. It does not seize us all at once. Down and still down it comes; it has already buried its edge a full inch in the spirit before the sensations grow indistinct and confused; and still—slowly, but very perceptibly—it descends, until you wake one day drained, uninspired, severed from the work you loved. Disease, a fatal disease of the will, fell like the simoon upon your craft, disturbing, in a manner the most subtle and terrible, the very identity of your passion. You will say, of course, that it is permanent; but not so. In what follows I shall trace its causes, name its warning signs, and set down the practical means by which the soul is led back to its best work.
