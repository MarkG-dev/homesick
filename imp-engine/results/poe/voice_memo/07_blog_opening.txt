I will testify to it, because most of us meet it sooner or later: creative burnout, that slow and quiet ruin of the working soul. It does not announce itself. It comes on by degrees—so gradual, so perceptible only in hindsight, that you do not name it until you wake one morning drained, uninspired, estranged from the very work you once loved. In an age that measures a person by what they ceaselessly produce, the toll of this is real, and heavy, and not to be waved away. But hear me out, for this is not a thing without remedy. Burnout need not be your tomb. In the pages that follow I mean to set down, plainly, what causes it, how to read the signs of its approach, and the practical means by which a worn soul is led back—back to its passion, and back to its best work.
