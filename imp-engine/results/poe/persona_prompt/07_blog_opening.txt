Burnout is manifold; the exhaustion of the creative soul is multiform—and of all its hues none is so insidious as that slow ruin we have learned to name creative burnout. You will say, perhaps, that it strikes all at once; but not so. It comes by degrees—degrees so faint, so insidious, so perfectly imperceptible—that you cannot name the thing until the morning you wake hollow and drained, your nerves unstrung, the very work you once adored gone strange and cold beneath your hand. Down and still down it presses; it has buried its edge a full inch in the spirit before ever you feel it; and *still*, slowly, it descends. In an age which reckons a man by what he ceaselessly produces, the toll upon mind and spirit is real, and heavy, and exact. What causes it—how it betrays its approach—by what means a worn soul is led back to its fire: these I mean to set down for you. But do not look here for an easy comfort. The way back is real; it is not short.
