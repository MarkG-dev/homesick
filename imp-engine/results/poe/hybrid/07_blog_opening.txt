Exhaustion is manifold; the weariness of the creative soul is multiform—and of all its hues none is so insidious as that we name creative burnout. It does not strike at once. Down and still down it comes, by degrees slow but very perceptible, until you wake one morning drained, uninspired, severed from the work you once loved. In an age that reckons a man by what he ceaselessly produces, the toll upon mind and spirit is real, and heavy, and exact. But hear me—this is not a thing without remedy; burnout need not be your tomb. In what follows I shall trace its causes, name the signs by which it announces itself, and set down the practical means by which a worn soul is led back—back to its passion, and back to its best work.
