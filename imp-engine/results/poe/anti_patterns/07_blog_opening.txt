You imagine burnout arrives as a single blow. It does not. Hear me, for I have felt its cold hand at my own throat: it comes by degrees—degrees so faint, so insidious, so perfectly imperceptible—that you cannot name the thing until the morning you wake hollow, drained, your nerves unstrung, the very work you once adored gone strange and cold beneath your hand. Down and still down it presses; it has buried its edge a full inch in the spirit before ever you feel it; and *still*, slowly, it descends. In an age that measures a soul by what it ceaselessly produces, the toll is real and heavy and exact. What causes it, how it betrays its approach, by what means a worn soul is led back to its fire—these I mean to lay bare. But do not look here for easy comfort; the way back is real, and it is not short.
