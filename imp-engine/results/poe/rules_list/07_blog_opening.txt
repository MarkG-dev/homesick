Exhaustion is manifold. The weariness of the working soul is multiform—and of all its hues, none is so insidious as that which we have learned to call creative burnout. It does not fall upon you in a single stroke. No: it comes down, and still down, by degrees slow but very perceptible, until one morning you wake to find the work you once loved gone strange and cold beneath your hand, yourself drained, uninspired, severed from the very thing that made you. In this our restless and merciless age, where to cease producing is accounted a kind of death, the toll upon the mind is ghastly and exact. Yet attend me—for this is no tale that should not be told. Burnout need not be your sepulchre. In what follows I shall trace its causes, name for you the warning signs by which it announces itself, and set down the precise measures by which a soul, however unstrung, may be brought back—back to its passion, back to its truest work.
