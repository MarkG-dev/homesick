Subject: A proposition you'll see through immediately

My name is Alex. I'm reaching out about a partnership — the word, the ask, and the awkward silence it drops into your inbox like a wet trout onto a clean desk.

Here's the thing. We've been watching what you've built. Not the way surveillance watches, not the way a competitor watches, but the way you watch something you respect from across a parking lot at 11 p.m., knowing you're not quite ready to walk over. Your work has an audience that trusts it. So does ours. Those two facts are sitting next to each other and not doing anything useful yet.

I think they could.

A call, twenty minutes, sometime next week — that's all I'm proposing. No deck, no funnel, no carefully rehearsed pivot when you say you're not interested. You'd ask what we're after, I'd tell you, and we'd find out fast whether this is worth either of our time or whether it dies the quiet death most of these emails deserve.

I'm aware this pitch reads like every other pitch. The city is full of them, sliding under doors, pooling in inboxes the way rainwater pools in a cracked sidewalk — going nowhere, meaning nothing, patient in the way only useless things are patient.

I'm pitching anyway.

If any part of this lands, reply and we'll find a time.

Alex
