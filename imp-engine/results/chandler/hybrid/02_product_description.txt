The Aura Pro noise-canceling headphones. Thirty hours of battery life, active noise cancellation that actually means something, and sound clean enough to make you forget what city you're sitting in — which, depending on the city, is either a luxury or a mercy.

The noise cancellation works the way a closed door works, the way a closed door works when the argument on the other side of it is the whole world and you've finally stopped caring who wins. It doesn't muffle the racket. It erases it, the way morning erases a bad dream, except this one stays erased.

The cushions sit against your ears like they were made for them, which they were, and the sound that comes through is the kind of clear that costs money and admits it without apology. Highs that don't flinch. Bass that knows where to stop. A midrange that tells the truth even when the truth is complicated.

You can wear them on a plane, at a desk, in a waiting room that smells of fluorescent light and postponed decisions. The Aura Pro doesn't care where you are. It cares what you hear. There's a difference, and it's worth every dollar of the price tag — which, unlike most things in this world, is the only thing about it that's straightforward.
