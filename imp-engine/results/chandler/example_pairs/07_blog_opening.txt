Creative burnout doesn't announce itself. It shows up the way most bad things do — gradually, then all at once, until one day you're staring at a blank page that used to feel like possibility and now just feels like an accusation. The work you loved starts to feel like work you owe, and there's a difference between those two things that no amount of productivity advice can paper over. The pressure to keep producing, keep shipping, keep being brilliant on a schedule, has a way of hollowing a person out without ever leaving marks you can point to. But burnout is not a verdict. It's a condition, and conditions have causes, and causes, once you understand them, tend to have remedies. That's what this post is about — where creative burnout comes from, how to recognize it before it takes everything, and what you can actually do to find your way back to the work that used to mean something to you.
