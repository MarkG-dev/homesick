The Aura Pro showed up on my desk on a Wednesday, which is just a Tuesday that's given up pretending things will improve. I put them on anyway.

The noise cancellation hit like a door closing on the whole rotten city — the traffic, the arguments, the low-level hum of a world that never learned to shut up. Industry-leading, they call it. I call it the first quiet I'd had in years, the kind of quiet that sits in a room the way a secret sits in a room, heavy and still and not going anywhere.

Thirty hours of battery life. Long enough to outlast most problems, though not the ones that matter. The fit was easy on the ears, the way good news is easy on the ears — you keep waiting for the catch, and it doesn't come.

The sound itself was clean. Honest. It carried the music straight to you without dressing it up or watering it down, the way a good bartender pours without editorializing. Every note landed where it was supposed to land.

Working, traveling, sitting in a rented room watching the neon bleed down the window — the Aura Pro didn't ask questions. It just played the music.

That's rarer than it sounds.
