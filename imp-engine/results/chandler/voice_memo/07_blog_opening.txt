Creative burnout had been following me for weeks before I finally turned around and looked at it. It moves like that — quiet, patient, the way a debt collector moves when he already knows where you live. One morning you're doing the work you love. The next, the blank page sits there like an accusation, and the ideas that used to come easy have packed their things and left no forwarding address. The city outside keeps grinding, keeps demanding, keeps sending the memo that productivity is the only virtue worth having. It takes a toll. It takes more than that, if you let it. But burnout isn't a life sentence — it's a symptom, and symptoms have causes, and causes, if you're willing to look at them straight, have solutions. So that's what we're going to do here: figure out where this thing comes from, learn to spot it before it hollows you out completely, and find a few ways back to the work that used to mean something to you.
