I burned out so completely last spring that I sat in front of a blank canvas for three hours and the most creative thought I managed was *maybe cereal for dinner*. Not a metaphor. Actual cereal. That's where creative burnout takes you — from someone who once couldn't sleep because ideas were clawing at the inside of your skull, to someone rationing the mental energy required to choose a spoon. It's not a slow fade, not really. It's a light that flickers, flickers, then one Tuesday it just quits. The room doesn't look different. You look different in it. And the work you used to chase like a dog after a truck — the work that felt urgent and electric and *yours* — starts to feel like something you read about happening to someone else. But here's what I've learned crawling back from the ash heap more than once: burnout is a diagnosis, not a sentence. In this post I'm going to show you what actually causes it, how to catch it before it catches you, and the specific, unsexy, genuinely effective things that brought me — and can bring you — back to life.
