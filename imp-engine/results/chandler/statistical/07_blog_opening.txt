Creative burnout had a way of finding you whether you looked for it or not. It came on slow, the kind of thing a man could miss for weeks, and then one morning he sat down at his desk and there was nothing there. The work he once wanted to do felt like something that belonged to someone else, somewhere far off. The pressure to keep producing never let up, and that kind of pressure broke things in a man — broke the quiet part where the good ideas lived. But it did not have to stay that way. In this post we looked at what caused creative burnout in the first place, the warning signs to find before they found you, and the kind of practical things you could do to get back across that distance and do the work you knew you had in you.
