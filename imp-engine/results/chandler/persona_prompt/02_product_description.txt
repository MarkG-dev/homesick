The Aura Pro arrived on a Tuesday, which is the kind of day that needs all the help it can get. Noise-canceling headphones. Thirty hours of battery life. Active noise cancellation that the engineers call industry-leading, which means it works well enough to make you forget there's a world outside — the traffic, the arguments through thin walls, the city doing what the city always does, which is talk too loud about nothing that matters.

Put them on and the whole ugly symphony drops away like a man who's been paid to leave. What's left is just the music, clean and close, the way sound was before someone decided to sell you the noise along with everything else.

The cushions are the kind of comfortable that makes you suspicious. The clarity is the kind you don't argue with. Thirty hours means you can board a plane in Los Angeles, land somewhere that isn't Los Angeles, and still have battery to spare — which, depending on your situation, is either a luxury or a necessity.

The Aura Pro won't fix anything. But it'll give you a quiet in which to think about what needs fixing. In this city, that's worth more than it sounds.
