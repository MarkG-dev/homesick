Creative burnout doesn't announce itself. It doesn't knock. It just shows up one morning the way a bad tenant does — already inside, already comfortable, already eating your food. One day you're working, the next you're staring at a blank page the way a man stares at a wall he's been staring at so long he's forgotten there used to be a door in it. The work you loved starts to feel like something you owe, and debt has a way of making everything ugly. The city outside keeps moving — deadlines, feeds, the relentless churn of everyone else apparently producing, apparently thriving, apparently not hollow behind the eyes — and you sit there wondering when exactly you traded your instincts for a schedule. But here's the thing about burnout: it's a condition, not a verdict. What kills it, what causes it, how to see it coming before it has you by the collar — that's what we're going to talk about. Consider this the part where you stop pretending the wall was always there.
