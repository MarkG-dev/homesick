It does not announce itself, the burnout; it comes the way evening drains the colour from a room before one has marked the light going — and one sits on at the desk, doing the loved work, and finds it has turned grey in the hands. Not unhappiness, exactly; something emptier; the gladness leaked out, the self gone far off and oddly estranged from the very thing that once made the morning worth its waking. And the world will not stop its asking — produce, it murmurs, and now another deadline, and now another — and something in one, some small green stem, bends, and bends. It need not break for good, though; that much is true. So here we shall go looking — for where the drained feeling truly begins; for its first quiet signs; and for the small ways back to the work one did, once, and may yet do again, the green stem lifting.
