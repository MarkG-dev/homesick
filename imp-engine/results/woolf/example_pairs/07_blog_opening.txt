It came on slowly, the burnout; the way the light leaves a room before one has marked its going; and one sat on at the desk, doing the loved work, finding it ash. How drained, how far off, stranger than this of course, the feeling was; like the flap of a wave; the kiss of a wave; chill and sharp and yet, in the fast-pressing world that cries produce, and produce, a real weight on the mind. But the burnout was not simply one thing, and not for ever. It was this, then, that we would go looking for — not the great collapse, but the small green stem bending; where it comes from; how to know it early; and the quiet ways back to the work one did, once, and may do again, at its best.
