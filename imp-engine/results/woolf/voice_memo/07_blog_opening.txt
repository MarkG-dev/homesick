It comes on slowly, the burnout — you are at the desk, doing the work you used to love, and you notice, the way you notice the light has gone from a room only once it is half-dark, that the gladness has leaked out of it; drained, you think, that is the word, drained and far off and oddly estranged from the very thing that once made the morning worth its waking. And the world will not stop its asking — produce, it says, and now another deadline, and now another, the bell striking and striking — and something in you, some small green growing stem, bends under it and bends and seems about to snap. But it need not snap for good; hold onto that. So here, together, we shall go looking — at where the drained feeling really comes from, at how to catch its first quiet signs, at the small patient ways back to the work loved again, to the self that did, once, and will again, its best.
