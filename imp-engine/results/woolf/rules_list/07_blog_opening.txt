It comes slowly, the burnout, the way the light leaves a room before you notice it has gone — and one sits there still, at the desk, doing the work one loved, and finds it has turned to ash in the hands; drained, that is the word, and uninspired, and oddly far from the very thing that once made the morning worth its waking. For the world will not stop asking (and now another deadline; and now another); produce, it says, produce — and something in the mind, some small green stem of it, bends and bends and threatens to break. But it need not break for good. Here, then, we shall go looking: at where the thing comes from, at how you might catch it early, at the small ways back — the squeak of the old hinge, the work loved again — to the place where you did your best.
