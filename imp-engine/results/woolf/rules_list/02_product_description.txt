Consider the Aura Pro, our finest yet, set down upon the ear like a hand laid over noise; for what it does, simply, is take the world's clamour away — the train's grind, the office hum, the stranger's cough — and hold it off, the way a wall holds off rain. And then the music comes in: clear, entire, each note its own small thing — like the flap of a wave; the kiss of a wave; chill and sharp and yet solemn. Thirty hours it will run before it tires (and one forgets, putting them on, that they sit there at all, so soft against the head). Working, travelling, doing nothing in particular by a window — it does not matter; the sound arrives, layered, near, the way it was always meant to, before the world got loud. That, perhaps, is all one wants of a thing: that it should give the music back, and take the rest away.
