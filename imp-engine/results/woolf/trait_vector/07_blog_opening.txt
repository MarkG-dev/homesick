It comes on slowly, the burnout, the way evening drains the colour from a room before you have noticed the light was going — and you sit on at the desk, doing the very work you once loved, and find it gone strange and grey in your hands, the gladness leaked out of it, yourself drained and far off and oddly estranged from the thing that used to make the morning worth its waking. For the world will not stop its asking — produce, it murmurs, and produce again, faster, and now another deadline, and now another — and something in the mind, some small green growing stem of you, bends under it and bends and seems about to snap. But it need not snap for good; that is the thing worth holding onto. So here, together, we shall go looking: at where this drained feeling truly comes from, at how to catch its earliest signs before they thicken, and at the small patient ways back — to the work loved again, to the self that did, once, and will again, its best.
