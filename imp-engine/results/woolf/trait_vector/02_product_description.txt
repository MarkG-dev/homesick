The Aura Pro arrives, our most accomplished yet, and what it offers is not really a feature at all but a kind of mercy: it takes the roar of the world — the engine's drone, the carriage's rattle, the murmur of a hundred strangers each sealed in a separate life — and lifts it away, so gently you scarcely feel the lifting, until there is only the music left, near and clear and entire, like a voice that has at last stopped having to shout. Thirty hours it will keep this up before it asks for rest, and all that while it lies against the head so softly that one forgets it is there, forgets there was ever noise to be spared. Working, or moving through the long grey corridors of travel, or simply sitting still in the late light doing nothing — it does not matter which; the sound comes through layered and faithful, the way, you remember now, sound was always meant to come, before the world grew loud and you grew used to it.
