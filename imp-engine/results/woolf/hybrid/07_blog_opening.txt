It comes on slowly, the burnout — you are at the desk, doing the loved work, and you notice (the way you notice the light has gone from a room only once it is half-dark) that the gladness has leaked out of it; drained, that is the word; drained and far off and oddly estranged from the very thing that once made the morning worth its waking. And the world will not stop its asking — produce, it says, and now another deadline, and now another — and something in you, some small green growing stem, bends under it, and bends, and seems about to snap. But it need not snap for good. It was this, then, that we would go looking for — not the great collapse, but where the drained feeling truly comes from; how to know it early; and the quiet ways back to the work you did, once, and may do again, at its best.
