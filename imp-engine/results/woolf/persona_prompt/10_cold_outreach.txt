I am Alex, and I write to you as half a stranger, which is the awkward truth of every such letter and worth admitting at the outset rather than disguising in warmth. A thought has been forming in me for some while — and a thought that will not settle is, I have learned, usually worth attending to — that your work and ours, set side by side, might come to something neither of us could quite reach alone. We have watched what you have built, longer than this note lets on, and the admiration was the unforced kind, the kind that arrives before one has decided to feel it and is therefore to be trusted. I will not pretend to a value I cannot yet describe; I can only say I suspect it is there, between us, waiting. Would you talk, briefly, next week — a short call — and let the half-formed ideas turn over between us? I should like to hear what you are reaching toward, and to set beside it whatever we might bring. I shall wait, with more hope than is strictly professional, to hear from you.
