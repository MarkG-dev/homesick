Your invitation to Thursday's planning meeting reached me, and the first thing I felt — before any reasoning had a chance to dress it up — was the small unguarded warmth of being thought of, which one undervalues precisely because it is so quiet. I had nearly said yes inside my own head. But the day, when I looked at it honestly, was already given away, the hours promised elsewhere and not to be unpromised; and so I must decline — not the gathering itself, which I should have liked, but only the arithmetic of the clock, which never negotiates. Would you let me have the notes afterward? I find I do not want to lose the thread of what you are all making, even from this distance. And another hour will come, a calmer one, for the two of us to talk properly. To be thought of is no small thing — and so I thank you, truly, with more than the usual weight the word is asked to carry.
