There are editors who write introductions like this every week: warm, vague, addressed to no one, promising everything. The words change; the content does not. "Productive and fulfilling," "informed and inspired," "exciting developments" — each phrase does the work of saying nothing while sounding like something was said. I have come to believe that this kind of language is not laziness. It is a decision. Plainly, it keeps the writer safe and the reader at arm's length.

This newsletter tries to do something different. Not inspire you, but show you a thing clearly. Not gather "insights," but name who made a choice, what it cost, and who paid. Each week I pick the stories that seem to me worth your time: the ones with a real person at the center, a concrete consequence at the end, and no comfortable language left to hide behind.

That is what this issue is.
