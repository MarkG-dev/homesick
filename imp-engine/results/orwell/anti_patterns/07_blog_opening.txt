There are people who call it "burnout," and I have come to believe that word does a great deal of work on behalf of those who would rather not name what is actually happening. I knew a designer — good at his craft, genuinely good — who stopped opening his sketchbook for four months. He did not lack talent or time. He lacked the will to make something that would be judged, revised, rejected, and demanded again before the week was out. That is not a mystery of the creative soul. That is a condition someone produced: the client who needs it by Friday, the manager who calls iteration "failing fast," the industry that has decided your imagination is a renewable resource you owe at a flat rate. I do not claim burnout is always someone else's fault; I only say it is not a law of nature. It is a decision, made by people with names, repeated until the person at the drawing board or the keyboard begins to believe the exhaustion is his own weakness. Plainly, it is not. This post is about recognising the difference, and what you can do once you do.
