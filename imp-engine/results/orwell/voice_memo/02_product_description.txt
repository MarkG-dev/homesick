The Aura Pro is a pair of headphones. That sounds like a modest claim, but follow it through. The noise cancellation does not merely reduce what is around you — it removes it. The hum of a train carriage, the open-plan office, the neighbor's television bleeding through the wall: gone. What remains is the thing you actually wanted to hear.

The battery runs for thirty hours. That is a full working day and most of the night. You will not be hunting for a cable at the airport.

The cushioning is designed to sit against the head without pressing into it, which is the difference between wearing headphones and forgetting you have them on. The sound itself is clear in the way that matters — not artificially brightened or thickened to impress you in a shop, but balanced, so that what you hear is what was recorded.

There is no particular kind of person this is built for. If you sit at a desk, it helps you concentrate. If you travel, it makes the journey bearable. If you simply want to listen to music without the rest of the world inserting itself, it does that too. The Aura Pro does not promise an experience tailored to your lifestyle. It promises that you will hear what you put into it, and that nothing else will get in the way.
