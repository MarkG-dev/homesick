There is a particular kind of encouragement that circulates among creative people, and it goes something like this: push through, stay consistent, show up every day. It sounds like advice. It is, in fact, a instruction to ignore what your body is already telling you. Creative burnout does not arrive like a breakdown — it arrives like a slow leak, the kind you do not notice until the tyre is flat and you are standing in the rain. The work starts to feel like lifting something wet. The ideas do not come, and when they do, they seem to belong to someone else. I do not say this to be dramatic; I say it because the warning signs are real and specific and most accounts of burnout bury them under words like *overwhelm* and *passion deficit*, which are words designed to make a plain problem feel complicated. What causes it, how to spot it before it hollows you out entirely, and what can actually be done about it — that is what this is for.
