I've been there. Staring at a blank screen, hands on the keyboard, waiting for something — anything — to come. The ideas that used to rush in like a flood had gone completely silent. That's creative burnout, and it doesn't knock politely before it walks through the door. It seeps in slowly, quietly draining the color out of work you used to love, until one day you realize you're just going through the motions. On one side, there's the relentless pressure to produce, to perform, to keep showing up with something brilliant. On the other, there's you — exhausted, hollow, disconnected from the very thing that once felt like your purpose. I know that gap. I've lived in it. But here's what I've learned: burnout is not the end of your creativity. It's a signal. In this post, I'm going to walk you through what actually causes creative burnout, the warning signs your body and mind are already sending you, and the real, concrete strategies that helped me — and can help you — find your way back to the work that matters.
