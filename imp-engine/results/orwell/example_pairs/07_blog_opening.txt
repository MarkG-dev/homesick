Creative burnout is not a mystery, and it is not a personal failing. It is what happens when the pressure to produce outlasts the conditions that make production possible — when the work that once felt like yours begins to feel like something you owe to someone else. It arrives quietly, as these things tend to do. One morning the page is blank and you are not troubled by it; a few months later the page is blank and you cannot remember what it felt like when it wasn't. The causes are not complicated, and neither are the signs, once you know where to look. What follows is an attempt to name both honestly, and to suggest some ways back — not to the version of yourself that never got tired, which never existed, but to the work itself.
