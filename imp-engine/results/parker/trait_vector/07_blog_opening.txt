Creative burnout isn't a gentle tap on the shoulder — it's a slow suffocation. One day you're lit up, ideas crackling like a live wire, and then somewhere between the deadlines and the demands and the relentless pressure to *produce something brilliant by Thursday*, the fire just goes out. You're still sitting at the desk. You're still opening the files. But the person who used to love this work? She's gone quiet. That's the cruel architecture of burnout — it doesn't announce itself, it dismantles you piece by piece until inspiration feels like a language you used to speak fluently and now can barely remember. Here's what matters though: burnout is not a verdict. It's a signal. And in this post, we're going to tear apart what causes it, how to catch it before it hollows you out completely, and exactly how to claw your way back to work that feels alive again.
