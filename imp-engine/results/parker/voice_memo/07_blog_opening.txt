Creative burnout arrives the way most disappointments do — gradually, and then with an air of having been perfectly reasonable about the whole thing. One morning the work that once seemed urgent and alive sits on the desk looking back at you with a kind of patient indifference, and you understand that somewhere between the deadlines and the deliverables, something quietly made other arrangements. The blank page does not taunt you. It simply waits, with all the time in the world. What follows is an attempt to examine how this happens, how to recognize it before you have already recognized it too late, and what, if anything, a person might do to find her way back to the work — not to the breathless infatuation of the beginning, perhaps, but to something steadier, and nearly as good.
