There was a woman who had once loved her work the way some people love a place they grew up in — not because it was perfect, but because it was hers. Each morning she sat down and something came, and she considered that ordinary, the way you consider breathing ordinary. Then one day nothing came. Then another day, nothing. She looked at what she was supposed to make and felt the way a person feels in a room where all the furniture belongs to someone else. This is what people call creative burnout, and it happens to more of us than would ever say so out loud. It comes slowly, which is part of what makes it so clever. By the time you recognize it, you have already been living with it for some time. But it is not permanent, and there is a way back. What follows is about how that way looks — what causes burnout, how to know when you are in it, and what other people have done to find their way to the other side of it.
