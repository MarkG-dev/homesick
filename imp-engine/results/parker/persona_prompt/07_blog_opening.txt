Creative burnout arrives the way most bad relationships do — gradually, then all at once, and with that same particular quality of making you feel it is somehow your fault. One day you loved the work. You were the kind of person who loved the work. And then you are sitting at your desk at two in the afternoon with a cup of coffee that has gone cold and a blank document that has the decency, at least, to be honest with you. The world, which has opinions about everything, has strong opinions about your output — more of it, faster, shinier, now — and the world does not ask how you are holding up, because the world has a deadline. What follows is an attempt to be useful about this: what drains the thing, how to know when it is drained, and what, if anything, can be done before you make a decision you will have to explain to your landlord.
