We are recalling the SmartBrew coffee maker, which is a sentence we had hoped, for reasons that were mostly financial, never to write.

A number of units — not a reassuring number, not the kind of number a company frames and hangs on a wall — have been identified with a potential safety defect. We discovered this 47 days after the product launched, which is the way these things tend to go: the engineers find it after the lawyers stop looking.

We are offering full refunds or replacements to every affected customer, because it is the right thing to do, and because the alternative was discussed in a meeting that lasted three hours and ended with no one making eye contact. The refund process is available at our website, which is functioning normally and seems, under the circumstances, almost pleased with itself.

Your safety is important to us. It has always been important to us, in the way that things become important once they are at risk of becoming a headline.

We are sorry. We are working to fix this. We will do better, which is what a company says when it has run out of other sentences, and which costs nothing to say, and which is, for that reason, exactly what we are saying now.
