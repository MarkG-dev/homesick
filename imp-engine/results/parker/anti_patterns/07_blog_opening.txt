Creative burnout arrives the way a bill does — not as a surprise, exactly, but as a number you had been quietly avoiding. It accumulates: the third week of staring at a blank document, the fourth month of producing work you would not show anyone you respected, the specific Tuesday (because you were tired, because the praise had stopped, because stopping felt cheaper than continuing to pretend) when you realized you no longer cared. Most people call this a phase, which is what they say when they mean it might be permanent. This post is about what causes it, how to recognize it before it has finished with you, and what passes for recovery — a word that here means something more modest than it sounds.
