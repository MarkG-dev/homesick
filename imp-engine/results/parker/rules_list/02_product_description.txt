The Aura Pro arrives in its box at 9 a.m. on a Tuesday, which is precisely when the open-plan office, the crying infant three rows back, and the colleague who has opinions about lunch reach their fullest, most committed expression of themselves.

Active noise cancellation — industry-leading, a fact the headphones are not modest about — does three things: it silences the office, it silences the flight, it silences the particular person you have paid thirty hours of battery life to avoid. Thirty hours, exactly, which is long enough to complete a transatlantic journey, a difficult conversation, and seventeen more difficult conversations you will now never have to have.

The ear cushions are soft. The sound is crystal-clear. The Aura Pro is, in this sense, a better companion than most companions, which is a observation the product makes without sentiment and the buyer absorbs without argument.

Premium. Tailored. Exceptional. These are the words one reaches for when one means: finally, a wall between yourself and everything that costs you something. The Aura Pro does not judge this. It simply delivers the music the way it was meant to be heard — cleanly, privately, by someone who has decided, for thirty consecutive hours, that the world outside can bill someone else.
