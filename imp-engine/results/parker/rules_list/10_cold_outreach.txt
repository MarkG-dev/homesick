Subject: A Proposal (Which Arrives, Unlike Most Things, With Good Intentions)

It is a Tuesday — or the kind of Tuesday that presents itself as an opportunity and is, in fact, a cold outreach email — and my name is Alex, which is to say I am the person responsible for what follows.

I have been watching what your company has built for some time now: the audience, the product, the quiet competence of it all, which is rarer than it ought to be and more expensive to acquire than anyone budgets for. I found it impressive. I still find it impressive. I mention this not because flattery is free — it isn't, one spends a certain credibility on it and hopes for a reasonable return — but because it is, in this case, accurate.

The proposal is straightforward. Our two companies could, between them, produce something that serves both our audiences rather than merely inconveniencing them. There are three ways such partnerships tend to go: one party extracts value, both parties perform enthusiasm for eleven months before quietly dissolving things, or — occasionally, at what feels like an unreasonable markup on optimism — something genuinely useful gets built. I am suggesting the third.

A call next week. Thirty minutes, which the calendar will pretend is nothing and which will, in fact, cost us both something we won't regret spending.

I'd like to hear what you're working toward. I have thoughts on where we might be useful to each other. The room, as they say, is open.

Alex
