Subject: A Proposition, Which May Interest You More Than Most

It is a Tuesday — or near enough to one — and this email has arrived in your inbox alongside, one imagines, seventeen others making precisely the same offer in precisely the same words, which is the sort of thing that happens to people who have built something worth noticing.

My name is Alex. I am reaching out because I believe our two companies could do something genuinely useful together, which is a thing I have thought carefully before saying, having spent considerable time with what you have built and finding it, against all reasonable expectation, impressive.

The proposition is this: our audiences overlap in ways that are valuable to both of us, our work complements rather than competes, and a partnership struck here could return more than either of us has invested — which is, when one thinks about it, the only kind worth making.

I would very much like a brief call next week. Not a long one. Twenty minutes, perhaps thirty, to hear what you are working toward and to share what we might offer in that direction.

You may, of course, decline to reply. Inboxes are full of good intentions and the people who meant to answer them. But I suspect you are not that sort, which is, after all, why I wrote to you first.
