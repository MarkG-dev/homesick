Creative burnout does not announce itself. It arrives the way most losses do — gradually, then completely — until one day you sit down to do the work you once loved and find that the chair is the most interesting thing in the room. The pressure to produce, to be consistently brilliant on a schedule, extracts its fee with great patience and very little mercy. The good news, such as it is, is that burnout is not a verdict. In this post, we'll look at what causes it, how to recognize it before it has finished with you, and what you might actually do to find your way back to the work — not the frantic, perform-for-the-algorithm version of it, but the real thing, the one you started doing before anyone was watching.
